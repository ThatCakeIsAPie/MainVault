# Faleth Capital — System Overview & Takeaways

## Core Mission
*"To eliminate the wage and salary model by creating and managing generational wealth."*

## The Argument
The wage/salary model was designed for an industrial era that needed interchangeable human parts. It pays for time, not value. It punishes efficiency. It severs the connection between effort and reward. The school system was built to produce compliant workers — bells, rows, convergent questions.

**The alternative:** Pay for value created. Let people own their results. Share the upside and the downside.

## Philosophical Foundation
- Rooted in Christian values — people are "handcrafted by the Almighty"
- Draws from the Talmudic one-third rule (land/business/liquid capital)
- Key principle: *"Make enough other people's dreams come true, and yours come true by default."*
- Treats people as adults with autonomy, capable of self-governance

## Three Paths Into the Ecosystem
1. **Build** — Join a subsidiary, earn contribution points, build equity through Class B shares
2. **Invest** — Open a Managed Capital Account (MCA), earn returns on pool growth
3. **Refer** — The Prosumer path: refer customers, earn 20% of their spend indefinitely, convert credits to cash or MCA contributions

## Compensation Architecture

### Layer 1: The Floor (Weekly)
- Modest guaranteed minimum — a soft advance against future earnings
- Exists to remove survival anxiety, not to be competitive compensation
- Opt-out available for supplemental earners

### Layer 2: The Value Share (Weekly)
- Per-unit contribution-based pay from gross margin
- Formula: Your Points ÷ Total Points × Unit Labor Pool
- Complete work faster = higher effective rate. No time clocks.

### Layer 3: The Ownership Stake (Quarterly)
- **Layer 3a:** 20% of subsidiary net profit, distributed by Layer 2 earnings
- **Layer 3b:** 20% of Faleth Capital consolidated net profit, distributed portfolio-wide
- Both can be taken as cash or minted into Class B shares

## Equity System
- **Class A:** Office members — 100% ownership, voting rights, non-dividend
- **Class B:** All participants — non-voting, dividend-participating, minted via profit share, burned on cash-out
- **MCA:** External investors — proportional co-ownership of the unified pool, 2-and-20 fee structure
- Book value = (Office Equity − Liabilities) ÷ Total Class B Shares Outstanding
- Dilution factor reported quarterly — measures growth velocity

## Governance
- Ranked Choice Voting (STV) for Lead elections — proportional representation
- Confidence votes — any participant can call one at any time
- Quarterly recalibration — team collectively governs point values, COF rates, floor amounts
- Lead cannot unilaterally change parameters, remove participants, or override team votes

## Scaling: The Cell Framework
- Subsidiaries grow through modular cells, not management hierarchy
- Each cell self-organizes under the Contribution Framework
- Two-layer point system: subsidiary-level (negotiated by Leads) + cell-level (set by cell team)
- Biological analogy: cells with clear inputs and outputs, not org charts with VPs

## Acquisition Philosophy
- Honor existing wages during transition
- Genuine observation time in a live subsidiary before asking anyone to join
- Generous severance for opt-outs
- Department-by-department rollout
- *"An acquired business is not a conquered territory. It is a group of people being invited into a better system."*

## Key Structural Features
- **Structural redundancy:** Not every layer needs to perform simultaneously — at least one layer is always functioning
- **Anti-speculative design:** No market multiples, no discretionary inputs, book value only
- **Labor costs flex with revenue:** COF rate scales proportionally — no fixed payroll crisis
- **50/50 deployment discipline:** Always ~50% liquid, ~50% deployed into operating businesses
