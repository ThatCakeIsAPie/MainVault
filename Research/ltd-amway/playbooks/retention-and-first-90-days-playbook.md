# Retention and First 90 Days Playbook

## Job

Convert a new registration into a person with **belief, activity, and something to lose**—before opposing voices and empty calendars win.

## Hard truth from the room

- Out of every 4 sponsors: ~1 quits ≤3 months, 1 culture-only, 1 incubates, 1 runs (NM benchmark).
- ~10–15 of 100 signups stay in a meaningful sense (Colgrove); zero-inflated denominators poison industry stats.
- Joe Markiewicz field rule: if you do not get someone under a new IBO quickly, belief collapses and the leg dies—often inside a month in today's attention market. Early money helps; **skin in the game (a person under them)** locks responsibility.

## First 90-day sequence (builder)

### Days 0–7: reality and rhythm

1. Products in use (personal story starts).
2. Simple customer or sample path started (VCS / goodie bag / MyShop)—business becomes tangible.
3. Calendar: next meeting, next open, next follow-up—no vague "someday."
4. Communication rhythm with sponsor/upline set (when, how, what is reported).
5. Desire fuel on rails: 2–3 audios/day, reading block, association (Night Owl / FNL as non-optional for builders).

### Days 7–30: early proof

1. Help them **make something happen fast** (customer, open, sit-down)—opposing voices outnumber coach ~10:1.
2. Priority retention lever: **sponsor someone for them** or under them so they have reputation and responsibility on the line.
3. Teach tracking: conversations, opens, customers, personal use—not feelings.
4. Protect environment: new people are distractible; time-bound attention protection can be legitimate. Permanent suppression of all growth stories is not the same thing (see room-tension note).

### Days 30–90: selection

1. Sort the cohort honestly: quit / stick / do something / run hard.
2. Pour coaching into runners and incubators who still move.
3. Do not moralize quitters; replace volume and raise standards ("you sponsored someone who sucks—raise standards").
4. Aim Year-1 at **team + width to find builders**; Year-2 at **depth to Platinum shape**.

## Customer retention (PV engine, not a side hobby)

From Spring Forward 2026 and field practice:

- Sample boxes / VCS are **on-ramps**, not endless transactions.
- Optimize for **customer lifetime value** and ditto/repeat—service beats spray-and-pray.
- Personal touch: feedback after first use, branded follow-up, appreciation events, individual communication. BSM does not replace human follow-up.
- Personal circle PV (150+ early) creates confidence and Strong Start-style momentum when available.
- Customer work funds belief; sponsoring without product reality is hollow.

## What keeps people

| Keep | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Early product/customer wins | Proof the vehicle works |
| Someone under them | Skin in the game / responsibility |
| Communication with upline | Coaching loop leaves and returns |
| Association | Desire and standards caught |
| Clarity of next actions | Noise loses to a scoreboard |
| Gratitude + grit | Long-horizon stay power (Dale Jones) |
| Servant leadership culture | People stay for relationships and formation, not only pin |

## What kills retention

- Registration without a 3–5 day action bridge
- Sponsor vanity without development
- Defending the business instead of creating activity
- Empty pipeline → pressure on the few who said yes
- Chasing edification / attaboys over results
- Treating the room as social club without build agreement
- Identity capture or fear-selling that collapses under scrutiny

## Coach checklist per new IBO (weekly)

- [ ] Personal use / customers this week?
- [ ] Conversations and opens logged?
- [ ] Next sit-down on calendar?
- [ ] Someone under them or in process for them?
- [ ] Audio/read/association happening?
- [ ] Upward communication happened?
- [ ] Bottleneck named in one sentence?
