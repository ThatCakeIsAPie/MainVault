# Heroes — Customer Journey

Updated Nov 11, 2026.

This is the customer side of Heroes. These five **steps** (I–V) are not the Heroes team **Phases** 1–5, and they are not Faleth's five **steps**. The three stay separate.

Customers come first; the team grows from taking care of them well. The customer's transformation is the product.

## The five steps (customer side)

I. **Discovery.** Content sparks curiosity. The website's only job is to start a conversation: one "let's talk" button and a short form. No shop page. Ask: what made you reach out?

II. **The conversation.** Sit-down if local (default for the first 100), Zoom if not, messages only if already warm. Talk about their health, not products. One offer: register as an Amway customer and the at-home blood panel drops from $230 to $154 (verify at checkout). Register together on the spot. Ask: what do you wish was different?

III. **Your numbers.** Panel results come back explained; a clinician stands behind any recommendation. Check in during the 45-day Valdura window. Ask: what do you want to do about it?

IV. **The routine.** What the numbers call for becomes a monthly order, then Amway Plus. They buy because it's working. Ask: how do you feel compared to a month ago?

V. **The advocate.** They have a before and after and send people. The business conversation happens only if they bring it up, one-on-one. Ask: who do you know who'd want this?

## The three-question frame

Same frame as the IBO side, applied to health: How do you feel right now? How do you want to feel? What's in the way? ("not sure" is a fine answer, and often the panel is the next step.) The rep's job is the middle box: name one obstacle and connect them to the panel. One obstacle at a time.

## Rules

One ask per step. Ask what they want, note it, come back and ask how it's going. Identify and connect, don't prescribe (peptide or GLP-1 decisions belong to the clinician). Recommend only what we use. The door stays open; they can stop at any step.

## CRM fields

Customer record: name, contact, source, stage (I–V), now, goal, obstacle, date of last touch, next touch (date + the question to ask). If every record has a next touch, the one thing is being done.

Rep counters: sit-downs completed (toward 100), registered customers, panels ordered, monthly orders.

Source: conversation with Claude, Nov 11, 2026. Supplements the earlier Heroes block.

PRINCIPLE
Customers come first; the team grows out of taking care of them well. The customer's transformation is the product. Chick-fil-A line: "If we get better, our customers will demand we get bigger."

THE FIVE STEPS (customer side)
I. Discovery. They see my content (business and my own health) and get curious. The website's only job is to start a conversation: one button, "let's talk," with a short form (name, contact, what they'd like to improve). No shop page. Ask: what made you reach out?
II. The conversation. Sit-down if local (default while doing my first 100), Zoom if not, messages only if already warm. Talk about their health, not products. One offer only: register as an Amway customer and the at-home blood panel drops from $230 to $154 (verify at checkout). Register them together on the spot. Ask: what do you wish was different?
III. Your numbers. Panel results come back explained; a clinician is behind any recommendation. The numbers do the selling. Check in during the 45-day Valdura window. Ask: what do you want to do about it?
IV. The routine. What the numbers call for becomes a monthly order, then Amway Plus. They buy because it's working. Ask: how do you feel compared to a month ago?
V. The advocate. They have a before and after and send people. The business conversation happens only if they bring it up, one-on-one. Ask: who do you know who'd want this?

THE ONE THING
Same three-question frame as the IBO side, applied to health:
- How do you feel right now? (e.g., tired)
- How do you want to feel? (e.g., energized)
- What's in the way? ("not sure" is a fine answer, and the best case: the panel is the obvious next step)
The rep's entire job is the middle box: name the one obstacle precisely and connect them to the panel. The customer owns the top and bottom. One obstacle at a time; three problems turns the conversation into a product pitch. When one is solved and the next appears, that's step IV working.
Blood can come from the kit or from a lab like Quest sent to Avellum.

RULES
- One ask per step. Never sell three things at once.
- The one actionable thing: ask what they want, note it, come back and ask how it's going. Every step is that same move.
- Rep lane is identify and connect, not prescribe. Peptide or GLP-1 decisions belong to the clinician. The strength of the panel is that the body answers, not the rep.
- We use the panel and products ourselves; recommend only what we use.
- The door stays open: stop at any step.
