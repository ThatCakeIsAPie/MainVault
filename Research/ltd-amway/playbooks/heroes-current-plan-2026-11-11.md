# Heroes — Context Brief (Lyle Cole's Amway/LTD team)

Updated Nov 11, 2026.

This is the current Heroes plan. It replaces the Monday/Thursday weekly OS in [[ltd-amway/playbooks/heroes-weekly-os.pptx]].

## What it is

Heroes is Lyle's Amway (LTD system) team. The name comes from the hero's journey: each person is the hero of their own growth story, and the team is the dojo where they train. The dojo produces sellers, operators, networkers and social movers, who can also be affiliates for non-Amway products.

## Why it exists

Amway is Lyle's leadership dojo and a bridge, not the end goal. The long-term container is [[faleth-capital|Faleth Capital]], an evergreen holding company that produces owners, not employees. Heroes is one step in Faleth's [[concepts/foundational/five-step-sequencing-model|5-step plan]]. The dojo plus Faleth together make people bulletproof for step four, starting their own subsidiary.

## The goal

20 people each doing $5,000 a month in revenue. That's $100,000 a month, or $1.2M a year (over $1M ARR), which is about 23,000 PV, past Ruby. It's a real $1M+ business, not just a pin. At Ruby, personal income is roughly $10,000 a month, which feeds investments. This is a planning number, not an income claim.

## The path (gates are revenue)

1. **Begin:** order and use your own products. Read, listen to podcasts, spend time with the team and your mentor. This builds mindset and your own product story. (Field guide gate: your own product story.)
2. **100 sit-downs,** face to face, before social media. Reps beat head knowledge. (Gate: 10 customers, about $1,000 a month.)
3. **Social at scale,** once you know what sticks. Route business questions to your mentor. Sponsor people into the team instead of charging them: free for a year, and they only pay product cost. (Gate: $5,000 a month in revenue.)
4. **Lead and start your own subsidiary.** Run your own meetings. (Gate: 5 people building their own base.)
5. **Multiply** to about 20 builders. Goal: $100,000 a month as a team.

Throughout, people work on their subsidiary and Faleth Class B share work on their own time.

## The weekly loop

- Every 30 to 90 days with your mentor: where am I, where am I going, what's in the way? Set your own goal, then a weekly activity number.
- Round table, a minute each: my number, what I did, what worked, what got in the way, next week's number. Then open workshop time (calls, mentor time, hanging out). Attendance is optional.
- Missed goal: did the activity happen? If not, fix that. If it did, change the plan, not the person.
- Stretch, don't tear: beat your number 3 weeks running and raise it. Miss it 3 weeks and lower it or change the plan.
- The round table replaces the old Monday/Thursday rhythm.

## Team code

Fruit first. Customers before team. The door stays open. Give away what scales. Be here by choice.

## The numbers (Lyle's, exact)

- 3.43 BV per 1 PV.
- Retail margin does not become BV. BV is what's left after margin.
- Example: $5,000 revenue minus the 20% margin ($1,000) leaves $4,000 BV. That's about 1,167 PV, the 12% bracket. 12% of $4,000 is about $480. Add the $1,000 margin and that's about $1,480 a month.
- PV brackets: 100 = 3%, 300 = 6%, 600 = 9%, 1,000 = 12%, 1,500 = 15%, 2,500 = 18%, 4,000 = 21%, 6,000 = 23%, 7,500 = 25%, 10,000 = 27%, 12,500 = 29%, 15,000 = 31%.
- 7,500 PV is officially Platinum. 15,000 PV is Ruby. On the same math, about 7 people at $5,000 a month reach Platinum and about 13 reach Ruby.
- Override on a downline is the bracket differential, so it's thin early and grows toward Platinum.
- Teach only from numbers people can check against their own statements.

## Why this scales now

Amway's IBO contract changes take effect Nov 13, 2026. They remove the non-compete and open up social media. You still can't pitch the business much in public, but you can talk about products freely without a private account, and that's what makes this scalable. Business talk stays private and one-on-one, and only happens when someone asks.

## Funnel

Public content and product/affiliate links, then registered customer, then Amway Plus subscriber, then Valdura / Avellum Health. The business conversation comes only if they ask, always privately. Access tiers: content for everyone, messages for customers, deep one-on-one time for team.

## Who we're looking for

People who want agency and ownership, are coachable, have a mentor posture and could become future operators. Not pin-chasers, hype buyers, or anyone who needs guaranteed / passive / investment framing.

## Guardrails

No income claims, lifestyle hype, fake testimonials or invented results. The $ goals stay inside the team, not in front of prospects. Keep an open exit; nobody is pushed to go all-in.

- **Stewards:** Faleth ownership-track people who still run network marketing somewhere, in a separate peer room, with no cross-recruiting of each other's lines (being customers of each other is fine).

### Core principles

- Donations and free access are a conversion step, not a funding model. The funnel in front (reach, trust, momentum) is what most open-source projects never build. Example: DHH and Omarchy.
- Free access is unpaid customer acquisition. People who couldn't pay early often pay gladly later (the piracy-to-customer pattern).
- If it scales, make it free; charge for what doesn't scale (Hormozi). Examples: Red Hat, Supabase, Obsidian Sync.
- The open door: when people can leave anytime, they're more likely to stay (psychological reactance). "One group has customers, the other has hostages, and only one sends referrals."
- Word of mouth is the only marketing channel you own and the only one that compounds.
- Information is a commodity; results are the signal ("by their fruits"). Value lives in people with real results and transfers through close contact.
- Profit produces credible people, credible people give mentorship worth having, and enough of them together becomes the culture. Culture is an output of concentration, not something installed top-down.
- Simplicity is the moat: simple AND high output.
- New duplication is duplication of fruit, not of materials.
- A business is measured by profit. Amway shouldn't be the top of the funnel for ministry; it works better for discipleship after.
- Content: a personal brand covering health/wellness and business/entrepreneurship. Report what you're learning; don't preach.
