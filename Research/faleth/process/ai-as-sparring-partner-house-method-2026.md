# AI as Sparring Partner (House Method)

## Claim

The best operating stance toward AI is to treat it like a **sharp but fallible teammate you bounce ideas off**—not an oracle and not a junior you blindly trust. Like House MD with his team: adversarial brainstorming surfaces the right answer even when the first suggestions are wrong.

## Operating rules

1. **Bounce, don’t defer** — use the model to force articulation; you own the call.
2. **Expect dumbass moves** — hallucinations, overconfidence, and local optima are features of the loop, not reasons to abandon tools.
3. **Verification is the stop condition** — SOUL rule: tool proof > model self-report ([[faleth/process/agentic-loops-design-2026]]).
4. **Sparring improves your thinking** — the value is often the clarification you produce while arguing with the draft.

## GovCon hallucination stress test

A bookmarked meme shows an agent completing a pivot after misreading “25 new Subway locations” as “25 new subway stations,” then forecasting a $10 million government contract for the transit app it had begun building. Comedic, yes; fictional revenue remains stubbornly difficult to deposit.

The operational lesson is serious: **verify the opportunity before automating capture, proposal, or fulfillment**. In GovCon workflows, model output should resolve to primary evidence such as a SAM.gov notice, agency forecast, solicitation, amendment, or contracting-office communication before it can trigger code, spend, staffing, or pipeline value. The more autonomous the implementation loop becomes, the earlier this premise gate must sit. [[research/raw/x-bookmarks/2026-07-16/2077831023329476757]]

## Faleth / Hermes

- Matches planner/executor routing: expensive brain plans and **verifies**; fast models draft.
- Protects against both **tool worship** and **tool phobia** during GovCon + automation work.
- Pairs with cost/speed tradeoffs: cheap loops for bounce, premium only when verification needs it ([[faleth/process/frontier-model-cost-speed-tradeoff-2026]]).
