# Open Loops Cognitive Drain (2026)

## Claim

Chronic fatigue is often less about raw workload and more about **unclosed loops** running as background processes: unanswered texts, owed apologies, deferred decisions, postponed conversations.

## Distillation

1. **Open loops consume RAM.** Incomplete social and decision threads keep a low-level context switch alive all day.
2. **Workload ≠ cognitive load.** You can finish tasks and still be exhausted if the unfinished *relational/decision* set is large.
3. **Closure is recovery.** Completing, deciding, or deliberately parking a loop (with a next action) frees capacity more than another “productivity system.”

## Faleth / Lyle application

- **Transition season (layoff → VXE cash timing):** open loops include UE paperwork, partner follow-ups, budget-pending deals, family logistics, LTD invites. Treat them as a **queue to close**, not ambient guilt.
- **Agent ops:** same pattern as software — unfinished tickets and “I should verify that” items drain main-session attention. Prefer write-downs + stop conditions ([[faleth/process/agentic-loops-design-2026]]).
- **Daily practice:** end-of-day close 1–3 loops (reply, decide, schedule, or archive with a next step). Pairs with [[faleth/mindset/mindset-as-infrastructure-mental-diet-2026]] and [[concepts/offshoots/calendar-as-proof-of-priorities]].

## Guardrails

- Not medical advice; sleep, health, and overwork remain real.
- “Close the loop” is not people-pleasing speed-replies; honest decisions count as closure.
