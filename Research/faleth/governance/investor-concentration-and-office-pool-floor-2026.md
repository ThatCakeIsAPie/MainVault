# Investor Concentration Caps and Office Pool Floor

> Book-value pricing attracts more capital than the office can honestly deploy. Cap any one investor at 10% of the pool, keep the office at no less than 25%, and refuse the rest. Soft leverage is the thing being bought off.

This is a **proposed parameter fill**, not a ratified framework amendment. [[The Equity Framework]] already states the per-account cap as a principle and lists both its size and total MCA capital relative to the pool as still to be set. The 10% number lived in drafting conversation and did not land in the documents.

## Proposed parameters

| Rule | Number | What it actually rations |
|---|---|---|
| Individual MCA cap | 10% of the total unified pool | One account cannot become an informal veto |
| Office floor | 25% of the total unified pool | Outside capital cannot become the economic landlord |
| Implied MCA ceiling | 75% of the total unified pool | Fills the open "total MCA capital relative to the pool" slot |
| Price | Current book value, both directions | No market-clearing by raising the price |

At a 10% individual cap and a 25% office floor, fully using the outside room requires at least eight MCA accounts. Seven accounts at the cap would be 70%, leaving 5% residual or an office share of 30%.

The Financial Framework's pedagogical example of one investor at 60% of a $5 million pool would be illegal under these numbers. If the parameters are later written into the frameworks, that example has to change.

## Mechanism

Two complementary controls, not one:

1. **Price freeze.** Capital enters and exits at book value, not a market multiple. That is the "price control." The discrepancy versus a market price is the bait.
2. **Quantity ration.** Because the bait works, demand is expected to exceed the people and businesses that can produce the return. The 10% and 25% rules refuse surplus subscriptions instead of letting price or concentration clear the market.

The office floor is also a **raise throttle**. MCA capital cannot exceed three times office equity. The next investor dollar is declined until retained earnings, performance fees, or other office equity grow the denominator.

Governance is already withheld from MCA holders. These rules target the leftover problem: informal leverage from a large economic slice, and a destabilizing exit from a position that was allowed to get that large. That is already the stated purpose of the cap in [[The Equity Framework]] section 4.

## Incentive diagnosis

Lyle's current read of the system:

- Capital should not be the scarce input. Book-value entry is too attractive for that.
- The scarce inputs are good people and good businesses, arriving fast enough to produce the returns the discounted entry implies.
- Therefore do not loosen ownership rules to raise money. Tighten them so money cannot buy a voice or a pace the office cannot keep.

This is a How/Talent constraint, not a How/Treasure constraint. More capital without more operators and more honest book-value businesses produces disappointed investors, not durability.

## Boundary

These numbers do not create returns. They only keep disappointed capital from becoming a constituency. If operator and business throughput stay slow, the honest move is a closed or slow subscription book, not a looser cap.

Legal structure, hurdle rate, minimums, and reporting remain unset and still require counsel before any MCA is offered. See [[The Equity Framework]] section 6.
