# One Billion Homes — DFW build cost model

**Open the interactive explorer:** [[raw/documents/dfw-build-cost-explorer-2026-11-23.html|DFW Build Cost Explorer]] (the original HTML, unchanged). That interactive page is the one Lyle uses; this page is only a map of it.

> **Caveat (from the explorer itself):** The NAHB survey figures are published. Many unit prices are the model's own estimates. Replace them with supplier quotes before relying on them.

## What it is
- Claude built it for Lyle on 2026-11-23 so he could see what it costs to build a house.
- It is the detailed cost breakdown behind the reference model in [[One_Billion_Homes]]: same NAHB 2024 average home, $665,298 sale price, $91,057 lot.
- Drill-down: sale price, then construction cost, then 8 stages, then each line, then quantity times unit price, with a source and a confidence level on every row.
- Underneath is a bottom-up model of one reference home in Dallas-Fort Worth, priced November 2026.
- Tabs: **Breakdown**, **Opportunities** (lines that are large, labor-heavy, or priced far above their materials), **Model notes** (home spec, labor rates, limits, sources).

## The reference home (model)
- 2,650 sq ft, two stories, 4 bedrooms, 3.5 baths, attached 2-car garage.
- Post-tensioned slab, 2×4 framing, hip roof with architectural shingles.
- Brick on the first floor, fiber-cement siding upstairs.
- Lot about 55 × 125 ft.

## Why it matters for One Billion Homes
- [[One_Billion_Homes]] says every trade brought in-house moves money from cost of goods into the crew's pool. The explorer shows where that money sits, line by line.
- Framing is the clearest example: most of the line is labor and markup, not lumber.

## Lyle's compensation ideas (2026-11-23, hypothetical)
He called these numbers a hypothetical example, not settled parameters. ^[raw/conversations/spar-2026-11-23-one-billion-homes-compensation-drip.md]

**What the explorer showed him**
- Leaving out land and fees, there is about a $400k gap between materials and the sale price. Labor is only about 39% of it.
- Even that labor figure is high next to what workers actually make per hour.
- He wants to find out what the slice the model can't explain really is.

**The stack**
- All work in-house.
- A floor for everyone, maybe non-credited at first.
- Work above the bare minimum earns well above the floor.
- Profit share on top. A project manager he knows says nobody he knows offers it, so it would attract and keep talent.
- Company trucks and tools.

**The drip (new idea)**
- About half of what someone earns on a finished home is paid monthly over a year, as long as there are no callbacks. A callback stops it.
- This is bigger than the current quality holdback in [[One_Billion_Homes]] §2, which holds back "a small share" and releases it at the one-year walkthrough.
- Who loses it: the crew whose stage failed loses that home's drip. If other departments could have caught a deeper problem, everyone loses it.
- Example: an apprentice electrician earns $4,000 on a home. $2,000 is paid in Layer 2; $2,000 drips at about $167 a month for a year. At 4 to 5 homes a month the drips stack, and they fade if the person stops working.
- Purpose, in his words: first quality. Second, it "silently gets people hooked on recurring income," which leads toward Class B dividends, then to building income-producing systems and family wealth.
- He is not married to the 40-hour weekly cap on Layer 2.

**Drip to Class B**
- Everyone gets gain share and chooses to take it or mint it. Most start by taking. Around people who mint, they see they could build equity, and they switch.
- Touches [[The Equity Framework]] §2 and [[The Contribution Framework]].

## How it starts (2026-11-23, his words via Spar)
^[raw/conversations/spar-2026-11-23-one-billion-homes-how-it-starts.md]
- **One cell first**, not a whole home.
- **One Billion Homes replaces the electronics manufacturer** he had planned with about $100-200k. He doesn't want to keep jumping between intermediary businesses.
- His frame: "what has to happen from here to there."
- Likely path: start as a **subcontractor** for a general contractor. Then use VXE resources and experience in the bottleneck to become the **general contractor**.

**His role, as he concluded it**
- He doesn't have to build the whole business himself. His job is to gather the team, secure the funding, and set the compensation plan.
- So the highest-leverage work may be networking: finding people, possible subs, and companies that could help raise money. Someone may know where the first tasks can start.
- One option: hire or partner with an experienced project manager who shares his vision.

**Habitat for Humanity** ^[raw/conversations/spar-2026-11-23-habitat-for-humanity-contacts.md]
- Reconnecting with Habitat doubles as people-finding: the people he talks to there can get him contacts.
- Same goal, different method, in his words:
  - Habitat lowers the price of each house it sells, and uses a contract to keep the profit if the owner resells soon after.
  - He keeps the profit up front, sells or rents like a normal general contractor, and builds so many homes that supply pushes housing prices down.
- This matches [[One_Billion_Homes]] §1 (sell at market; prices fall through supply). §7 already names Habitat affiliates as buyers from the Component Plant.
