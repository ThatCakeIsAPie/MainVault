# Contribution Compensation vs Wage Compensation

This comparison captures one of the central economic contrasts in the wiki: paying people for time/position versus paying them for value-producing contribution.

| Dimension | Wage / Salary Compensation | Contribution Compensation |
|---|---|---|
| Unit rewarded | Time, role, negotiated market rate | Value-producing contribution |
| Incentive problem | Efficiency can reduce hours paid; ambition can subsidize underperformance | Output, judgment, and effectiveness are rewarded directly |
| Risk allocation | Employer carries fixed payroll obligation | Payout flexes with actual value/revenue |
| Transparency | Often opaque and negotiated individually | Intended to be calculable and visible |
| Governance | Usually imposed from management | Designed to be governed by contributors |
| Failure mode | People optimize for doing the least while preserving pay | Measurement system can become political or overcomplicated if poorly designed |

## Effective Effort

The raw "effective effort" frame sharpens the distinction. Effort alone is not value; effort becomes economically meaningful when combined with skill, judgment, coordination, risk-taking, and capital allocation. A system that pays only for time cannot distinguish productive effort from expensive motion. ^[raw/transcripts/Trust_Effective_Effort_and_The_Factory.md]

## Synthesis

The contribution model is not simply "pay harder workers more." It is an attempt to reconnect reward to value creation while making the rules transparent enough that contributors can govern the system themselves.
