# Factory Thinking vs Product Thinking

Product thinking focuses on the visible output. Factory thinking focuses on the system that repeatedly creates outputs.

| Dimension | Product Thinking | Factory Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | The offer, deliverable, product, or project | The repeatable system that produces offers, deliverables, people, and projects |
| Time horizon | Immediate output and revenue | Compounding capacity |
| Primary question | "What are we shipping?" | "What produces this reliably without heroic effort?" |
| Failure mode | Permanent dependency on the founder/operator | Overbuilding systems before validating the product/use case |
| Best use | Early validation, fast learning, proof of value | Scaling after a pattern has proven worth repeating |

## Core Insight

A product generates revenue; a factory generates products. The deeper leverage is therefore not merely creating a successful artifact, but building the process, team, culture, and trust layer that can create many artifacts. ^[raw/transcripts/Trust_Effective_Effort_and_The_Factory.md]

## Connection to Network Marketing

Tyler Sheridan's "leveraged leadership income" frame is a people-development version of factory thinking: the builder earns as others win because the system develops additional owners rather than leaving one operator to personally produce everything. ^[Business/LTD Amway/Info Sessions/2026/2026-06-04 Tyler Sheridan.md]

## Practical Implication

The sequence matters. First build the product manually enough times to understand the pattern. Then convert the repeated pattern into a factory. Automating or delegating too early creates a brittle machine around an unproven process — extremely efficient stupidity, humanity's proudest recurring invention.
