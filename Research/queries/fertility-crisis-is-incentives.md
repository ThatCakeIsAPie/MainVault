# Fertility Crisis Is Incentives

Lyle's claim: the fertility crisis comes down to incentives. There is no incentive to have more kids.

## Household claim

- Well-off families are also below replacement, so money is not the limiting factor in the way housing-as-boomer-passive-income stories imply.
- After the farm-to-city shift and the end of child labor, children consume until they leave. They make parents' lives harder economically, not emotionally.
- For most of history, kids *were* the retirement strategy: parents raise children, then those children provide for the parents. 401(k)s and Social Security moved that claim onto other people's kids (the 40/40/40 plan). Grown children are now expected to strike out on their own, not repay in kind.
- Result, on a purely economic lens: kids add nothing and take everything. The incentives were engineered that way.
- Church-going replacement fertility is a cultural value, therefore a weak incentive. Individuals are unpredictable; groups are predictable; money is one of the strongest group incentives. Without enough concentrated energy (a thermostat, not an endless purity spiral), groups drift back to economic incentives.

## Civic claim

The required repair is not a single cash transfer. It is restoring the [[offshoots/family-formation-demographic-flywheel]]:

1. Good jobs
2. Early marriage
3. Inexpensive housing
4. Children
5. Community growth
6. Schools and family-serving demand
7. More jobs
