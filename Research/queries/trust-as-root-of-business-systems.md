# Trust as Root of Business Systems

## Short Answer

Trust is the root asset under business systems because it lowers coordination cost. When people trust the operator, incentive structure, process, and shared mission, they contribute effort without every interaction requiring defensive verification.

## The Mechanism

The raw source defines trust as the belief: "If I invest my effort here, I will not regret it." That belief allows people to contribute attention, effort, money, reputation, and risk toward a future result that does not yet exist. ^[raw/transcripts/Trust_Effective_Effort_and_The_Factory.md]

## Where Trust Shows Up

- **Compensation:** people trust that reward follows contribution.
- **Mentorship:** mentees trust correction because the mentor's success is aligned with their growth.
- **Delegation:** leaders trust others to own outcomes after training.
- **Governance:** contributors trust the rules because they are transparent and participatory.
- **Sales:** customers trust the offer because the operator uses the product and has evidence.
- **Culture:** teams trust the environment because standards are consistent.

## Connection to Factory Thinking

A factory is not only process documentation. It is a trust-producing system. If the system develops people, rewards contribution, and repeatedly delivers value, trust compounds. If it obscures incentives, wastes effort, or exploits people, trust decays and coordination cost rises.

## Practical Question

When a business system is stuck, ask: "What trust is missing?" The apparent bottleneck may be leads, capital, hiring, sales, or execution, but the root may be that someone does not yet trust the person, the process, the payoff, or the mission.
