# Heart Upstream of Identity — Theological Horticulture

## Core Metaphor

Religion tries to force fruit onto a tree. Relationship changes the root, and then fruit becomes the natural output. Duct-taping apples to a dead branch does not make it an apple tree.

## Principle

Behavior is downstream from identity, but identity is downstream from the heart. What the heart actually loves, fears, worships, craves, or reaches for becomes the gravitational center that identity organizes around.

## Why Behavior Monitoring Has Limits

Behavior monitoring can be useful because repeated behavior can train identity: the more a person does something, the more they may come to see themselves as the kind of person who does it. But if the heart is not actually aligned, the behavior remains an uphill battle. The person is managing fruit while the root still wants something else.

## Terminology: Religion vs Relationship

In this framing, "religion" means a collection of behaviors, visible norms, and external compliance. It does not mean a denomination or the mere existence of doctrine. The contrast is between being a follower of Christ and being merely culturally or behaviorally "christian." This is why modern Christianity is sometimes criticized as "church-ianity": church behaviors can be present while the heart-level relationship is absent. ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-religion-as-behavior-collection-churchianity-2026-06-04.md]

## Durable Change

Durable change requires more than external compliance. The heart must begin craving the right thing so behavior becomes congruent rather than forced. In Christian language, this is the difference between mere religion and relationship with Christ: not behavior pasted onto the outside, but a transformed inner source producing different fruit.
