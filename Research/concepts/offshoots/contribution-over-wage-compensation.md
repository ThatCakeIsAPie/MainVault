Traditional wages and salaries break the direct connection between value created and reward received. A better system pays for contribution, not time.

### Rationale

Hourly wages penalize efficiency (the faster you work, the less you earn per hour). Salaries penalize ambition (the more you do, the more you subsidize underperformers). Both models treat people as commodities whose time is purchased. 

The Contribution Framework replaces this with a process-based model where compensation is tied to value created, transparency, shared upside/downside, and long-term thinking. It draws from pre-modern self-organizing groups (the "Mammoth Principle") where effort and reward were visibly linked.

### Examples from Practice

- Efficiency is rewarded rather than penalized: someone who generates the same value in 10 hours earns the same as someone who takes 40.
- Compensation is fully transparent and calculable by everyone at any time.
- A portion of earnings is tied to the long-term health of the organization, not just short-term output.
- The team governs the system itself — point values and payout ratios are decided collectively.
- The "effective effort" frame separates raw effort from value-producing effort: the market rewards effort only when it is combined with skill, judgment, coordination, risk-taking, and capital allocation. ^[raw/transcripts/Trust_Effective_Effort_and_The_Factory.md]
