# Move First and Over-Deliver — Others Mirror the Frame You Set

> **Status: hypothesis for Lyle to confirm.** This page names a thread Wiki Drop sees across Lyle's October 2026 shares. It is a synthesis, not a claim any single source makes.

## The pattern

In each source, the person who acts first sets the frame, and the other person tends to take the complementary position. If you give more seriousness, care, or effort than the moment seems to call for, the other person usually responds by easing off ("this is no big deal" / "this is too much").

## Supporting sources

- **Grievances (Jason Fried, via Jean-Louis Gassée's "two tokens").** Whichever token you take, "it's no big deal" or "it's the end of the world", the other person takes the other one. Dismiss a complaint and a mild annoyance becomes a crusade. Take it more seriously than they do and they are "almost forced to pick the 'It's no big deal' token." ^[raw/articles/jason-fried-no-big-deal-or-the-end-of-the-world-2026-10-28.md]
- **Lyle's generalization.** He sees this as a general people skill, not just customer service: whenever someone has a problem, take it more seriously than they do. He links it to Alex and Leila Hormozi's idea that "only one person can be in the angry boat" (his recollection; no source captured). ^[raw/conversations/lyle-note-2026-10-28-two-tokens.md]
- **Networking (Alex Hormozi, Acquisition.com).** Hormozi reviewed the sales scripts of every member of his networking groups "like it was an actual project that I got paid for," and people reacted with "this is too much dude." Each of those gave him a one-sided "IOU" that he later cashed in, for example when he needed help with Facebook ads. To reach people above you, don't ask "what value can I provide you." Figure out what you can do for them, do it, and hand it over. He also recommends about 20 minutes of homework before each call. ^[raw/transcripts/youtube-YuQ8ve1Amto-2026-10-28.md]
- **Giving with no guarantee of return.** Hormozi says some people never return the favor and "it is okay." Giving with nothing back still means "you just gave to someone," and that's the brand you would want anyway. Goodwill compounds across many givers. ^[raw/transcripts/youtube-YuQ8ve1Amto-2026-10-28.md]

## Why it might work (interpretation)

Over-delivering shows that you heard the person and value them. Fried's line "Everyone wants to be heard and respected. It usually doesn't cost much to do either" is the cheap side of the trade. ^[raw/articles/jason-fried-no-big-deal-or-the-end-of-the-world-2026-10-28.md] Hormozi's projects are the expensive side: real work, given before anyone asks. Either way, the other person no longer needs to escalate or bargain. This fits [[offshoots/trust-as-coordination-infrastructure]] and the "create value before extraction" step in [[offshoots/networking-and-connection-skills]].

## Possible second thread (weaker)

A few shares point inward rather than outward. They're about doing your own work deeply instead of performing for comparison: the Jobs-attributed "great people don't compete" quote (attribution unverified) ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-x-share-2108165794467074118.md], Björk's "if I only please myself, I might please ten people" ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-x-share-2105963013937205634.md], and Hormozi's remark that rented-Lambo posing "only impress[es] people who don't know what players look like" ^[raw/transcripts/youtube-YuQ8ve1Amto-2026-10-28.md]. This is a different idea from the main pattern, so it's listed separately.
