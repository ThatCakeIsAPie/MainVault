Alignment means voluntarily moving in the same direction as the team and upline without becoming a carbon copy or submitting to subjugation.

### Rationale

True alignment is presented as thoughtful participation and shared vision rather than blind obedience. It allows for individual style while maintaining unity of purpose. This distinction protects against cult-like dynamics while still enabling coordinated team effort.

### Examples from Practice

- "Alignment is not for you to be a carbon copy of your upline, thoughtless obedience. That is not alignment, that is subjugation."
- Leaders emphasize that different approaches can still be in alignment if the core direction is shared.
- People are encouraged to take ownership of their own growth within the aligned framework.
