# The shortest version

**Marketing is everything that turns "someone has a problem" into "they paid you to solve it, and they're glad they did."**

Not advertising. Not persuasion tricks. The whole path between two people who don't yet know about each other.

---

# Building it from zero

Imagine two people who have never met.

- **Person A** has a problem: their back hurts, their shop loses money on inventory, their kid is bored.
- **Person B** has something that genuinely helps.

They don't know each other exist. Nothing happens. No value is created, even though the solution already exists.

Marketing is the set of things that closes that gap. And the gap isn't one wall, it's seven. Each one is a first principle:

| # | Wall | The question in the buyer's head | What removes it |
|---|------|----------------------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Existence** | "I've never heard of you." | Awareness, reach |
| 2 | **Comprehension** | "What even is this?" | Clarity |
| 3 | **Relevance** | "Is this for someone like me?" | Positioning, targeting |
| 4 | **Belief** | "Sure, that's what they all say." | Proof, reputation, guarantees |
| 5 | **Access** | "Where do I buy it? This is a hassle." | Distribution, ease |
| 6 | **Worth** | "That costs more than it's worth to me." | Pricing, offer design |
| 7 | **Memory** | "I forgot about you by the time I needed it." | Brand, repetition |

Remove all seven and the sale happens almost automatically. Marketing is wall removal, in roughly that order. Everything else (campaigns, funnels, personas, channels) is a technique for knocking down one of these seven.

---

# The one equation

People don't buy because you persuaded them. They buy when:

$$\text{Perceived value} > \text{Price} + \text{Effort} + \text{Risk}$$

Marketing has exactly two moves: **raise the left side** (better product, clearer promise, more desirable, better proven) or **lower the right side** (cheaper, easier, faster, reversible, lower risk).

If you can move neither, no amount of clever copy will help. This is why marketing cannot rescue a bad product. It can only make more people find out about it faster, in both directions.

---

# What marketing actually includes

- Choosing **who** to serve, and who to ignore. A market is a choice, not a demographic.
- Choosing **what to make**. The product is the first and loudest marketing decision.
- Choosing **the price**. Price is a message about who it's for and how good it is.
- Choosing **where it's sold**. Shelf position, app store listing, salesperson, checkout flow.
- Choosing **what it says**, to whom, and how often.
- Choosing **what happens after the sale**, because that becomes what the next customer hears.

Advertising is one line item in that list. Most people mistake the part for the whole.

---

# The two jobs, in the end

1. **Be remembered.** When the need appears, you're the name that surfaces. Slow, compounding. This is brand.
2. **Be chosen.** When you surface, you win the comparison. Fast, measurable. This is conversion.

Remembered but never chosen is a waste. Chosen but never remembered means you rent every customer, forever.

---

# The math that keeps it honest

$$\text{Customer lifetime value} - \text{Customer acquisition cost} = \text{profit per customer}$$

Positive: you can afford to buy growth. Negative: you're paying to lose money faster. Marketing that ignores this is entertainment with a budget.

---

# Three rules of thumb

- **Selection beats persuasion.** Find people already trying to solve the problem. Manufacturing desire is expensive; relevance is nearly free.
- **Specific beats general.** "Best coffee in town" is noise. "Open at 6am for people who start work at 7" is a decision.
- **Truth compounds.** Word of mouth is the cheapest, strongest channel, and it's earned by the product, not bought by the ad. You can rent attention. You can only own a reputation.

<details>
<summary><strong>Where it gets more complicated (but not more fundamental)</strong></summary>

- **Funnels and funnels-of-funnels** are just the seven walls measured in sequence, with drop-off counted at each one.
- **Brand vs. performance** is the tradeoff between job 1 (cheap future sales) and job 2 (sales now). Companies usually over-fund whichever their dashboard shows most clearly.
- **B2B marketing** is the same seven walls, but with several people standing at each one, and longer gaps before the need appears.
- **Category creation** is what you do when the wall isn't "which one?" but "what is this?" It's the most expensive game and rarely worth it unless you must.

</details>

---

**In one paragraph:** Marketing is understanding what a specific group of people genuinely wants, building something real that serves it, making that obvious and easy and low-risk to obtain, and doing it at a cost that leaves money on the table. The product does most of the work. Marketing's job is to make sure the right people find out, understand, believe, can act, and remember.
