# Family Formation Demographic Flywheel

A high-fertility society is not produced by one subsidy or one housing story. It is produced by a self-reinforcing loop around young families. Lyle's 2026-08-24 instruction: treat the boom flywheel as the **required condition set** for the fertility problem as a whole, not as postwar trivia.

## Required conditions

These are the loop links. Break one and the wheel stalls.

1. **Good jobs** — earning power and a future that looks safe enough to form a household.
2. **Early marriage** — couples form young enough that several children are still biologically and socially available.
3. **Inexpensive housing** — ordinary families can buy or occupy space that actually fits children.
4. **Children** — the payload of the loop, not an afterthought once career and housing are optimized.
5. **Community growth** — more households in the same places (historically suburban buildout).
6. **Schools and family-serving demand** — children create local institutions and spending.
7. **More jobs** — that demand feeds back into employment, which restarts the loop.

Compressed: good jobs → early marriage → inexpensive housing → children → community growth → schools and consumer spending → more jobs.

## What this is not

- Not "household cash is the bottleneck." Well-off families are also below replacement. See [[queries/fertility-crisis-is-incentives]].
- Not a claim that recreating 1946–1964 one-for-one is possible or desirable.
- Not a license to treat ChatGPT's historical trigger list (soldiers coming home, the pill arriving in 1960, child-mortality collapse) as the same thing as these required loop links. Those were one-time or already-solved conditions. The flywheel is the repeatable ecosystem.

## Why it matters

If kids are net economic consumption for the household, the only groups that keep having them will be groups with a non-cash payoff (church, status, meaning, community) or a rebuilt economic loop that makes children valuable again at the family and civic level. Housing-only or wage-only stories fail because they touch one spoke and leave the wheel unmoved.
