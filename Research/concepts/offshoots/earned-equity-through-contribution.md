Equity should be earned through contribution and reinvestment, not purchased with outside capital.

### Rationale

A mint-and-burn share system creates equity that directly represents value someone has chosen to reinvest into the organization. There is no fixed pool of shares that can be bought or sold by outsiders. Every Class B share in existence was minted from earned profit share or reinvested dividends. This keeps financial ownership aligned with people who built value while allowing that earned stake to remain after active participation ends.

External capital can participate through a Managed Capital Account structure without gaining voting rights or diluting the earned equity of contributors.

The rewritten suite also separates rights by source. Class A represents earned stewardship and carries governance without financial dividends. Class B represents earned financial ownership and carries dividends without governance. Managed Capital Accounts represent outside capital and receive only the return produced by that capital, less fees. Money cannot purchase the rights attached to contribution or stewardship.

### Examples from Practice

- Class B shares are minted only when participants reinvest profit share or dividends.
- Shares are burned when participants cash out, maintaining the integrity of the earned equity pool.
- External investors use Managed Capital Accounts rather than buying shares, preserving governance for those who earned their position.
- Class B can be held indefinitely after departure; only new minting stops, so active contributors gradually gain relative weight without confiscating prior contribution.
- Minting and burning occur at book value, preserving the share-price ratio for people who stay.
