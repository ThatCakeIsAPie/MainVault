# Calendar as Proof of Priorities

A person's calendar reveals their real priorities more reliably than their stated intentions.

### Rationale

The notes frequently treat calendar discipline as evidence of seriousness. People say they want growth, but priorities only become real when they are scheduled, protected, and repeated. A full calendar is not automatically success, but an empty or chaotic calendar exposes lack of commitment, lack of planning, or false priorities.

This principle operationalizes [[offshoots/persistent-consistent-action]]: consistency needs a container, and the container is the calendar.

### Examples from Practice

- "How do you prove you are ready to win? Show your upline your calendar."
- "Business owners know how to starve the noise, but feed the priorities."
- "How can I work my calendar around it?" beats "How can I work it into my calendar?"
- "When you prioritize something, you put it on your calendar."
- Planning the day and week is presented as essential for people balancing family, work, and business.

### Why It Matters

This is a simple anti-delusion tool. If the stated priority is not on the calendar, it is probably a preference, not a commitment. Annoying, but annoyingly accurate.
