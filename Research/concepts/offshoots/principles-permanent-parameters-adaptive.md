# Principles Permanent, Parameters Adaptive

> **Protect the commitments that make the system itself; continuously recalibrate the numbers and methods that make it work here and now.**

## Rationale

Organizations usually fail in one of two directions. They freeze implementation details until yesterday's solution becomes today's obstruction, or they treat every commitment as negotiable until the institution no longer means anything.

The Faleth framework suite separates **principles** from **parameters**. Principles define the durable moral and structural promises: value rather than time, ownership earned rather than purchased, transparency, voluntary participation, portable earned stakes, book value in every direction, decision proximity, and value-preserving exit. Parameters are the context-sensitive dials: point values, COF rates, floors, fee percentages, Lead ratios, spending thresholds, referral rates, severance terms, cell boundaries, and transition timelines.

The first category provides identity and trust. The second provides adaptation.

## Examples from Practice

- Contribution points and COF rates are recalibrated quarterly, while pay remains tied to value rather than time.
- Cell boundaries may split or merge as workflow changes, while decisions remain local and interfaces explicit.
- Referral incentives vary by subsidiary economics, while customers who create value remain eligible to share in it.
- Acquisition timing and severance terms vary by deal, while surprise, coercion, and immediate pay cuts remain prohibited.
- Constitutional amendments can alter operating parameters but cannot abolish core transparency, mint-and-burn ownership, liquidity, or Peaceful Fork commitments.

## Diagnostic Use

When a system is strained, ask two questions:

1. **Is a parameter wrong for current conditions?** Recalibrate it.
2. **Is a proposed repair violating a durable commitment?** Reject or redesign it.

This prevents both bureaucratic fossilization and values drift.
