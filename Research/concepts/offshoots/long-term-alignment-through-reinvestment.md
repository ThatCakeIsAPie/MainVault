Participants should have structural incentives to think and act in the long-term interest of the organization by reinvesting a portion of their earnings back into it.

### Rationale

Short-term compensation encourages short-term behavior. By tying a meaningful portion of earnings to the sustained health and growth of the enterprise (through reinvestment into equity or long-term pools), people are incentivized to make decisions that benefit the organization over years, not just weeks or quarters.

This creates natural alignment between individual success and organizational longevity.

### Examples from Practice

- A portion of everyone’s earnings is structurally tied to the long-term health of the enterprise.
- Reinvestment into shares or the unified pool directly increases both personal return and organizational capacity.
- People who invest in the organization’s future are investing in their own future returns.
