# Value Creators Share in Value — Customers Included

> **When someone measurably creates value for the network, the system should return a share of that value to them—whether they contributed labor, capital, or relationships.**

## Rationale

Faleth treats a business as a network rather than a wall separating insiders from customers. Workers create and fulfill value. Investors supply deployable capital. Customers fund the system and can expand it through advocacy and referrals. These are different inputs, but each can create measurable economic value.

Traditional businesses commonly pay ad platforms and salespeople for acquisition while treating customer advocacy as free. Faleth applies the same contribution logic to customers that it applies to participants: referral value belongs on the acquisition line, and the person who created it should share in it.

This creates three ownership paths:

- **Build:** contribute labor and convert profit share into Class B ownership.
- **Invest:** contribute capital through a Managed Capital Account.
- **Refer:** contribute network value, accumulate credits, and potentially convert them into capital.

## Examples from Practice

- A consumer referrer earns a continuing percentage of the topline spend of customers they personally introduced.
- Referral value is recorded as cost of acquisition rather than treated as an extra distribution detached from ticket economics.
- Credits can first offset the customer's own spending, then become cash or—once legally available—Managed Capital Account contributions.
- The referral system remains one level deep because it is a customer-loyalty mechanism, not a disguised network-marketing plan.
- Subsidiaries adapt incentives to their actual purchasing cadence while preserving the value-sharing principle.

## Boundary Conditions

Value sharing follows measurable economics, not vague participation. Programs must fit the subsidiary's margins, customer cadence, tax treatment, and securities constraints. A poor product cannot be rescued by incentives; the product must earn advocacy first.
