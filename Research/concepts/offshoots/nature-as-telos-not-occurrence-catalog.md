# Nature as Telos, Not Occurrence Catalog

## Claim

Joseph Spurgeon's 2026-08-12 long note (bookmarked) separates two uses of "nature":

1. **Catalog sense:** whatever occurs in the world, including animal behavior.
2. **Teleological sense:** what a thing *is*, what its faculties are *for*, and therefore what counts as acting according to that nature.

The post's applied conclusion is that Christian talk of homosexual acts as "contrary to nature" uses sense 2, not sense 1. The durable idea for the wiki is the distinction itself, not a finished sexual-ethics treatise.

## Argument as stated

- Animal same-sex mounting is often misread by importing modern human identity categories. Temporary behavior can be dominance, bonding, competition, excitement, or misidentification rather than an "orientation."
- Even if some animal behavior resembles a human category, **occurrence is not ought**. Animals also eat feces, kill rivals, and eat their young.
- "Natural" here means the proper end (*telos*) of the faculties. The author argues male and female bodies are complementary and ordered toward a union that can produce children.
- Follow-up in-thread: the author treats polygamy as sin but "not unnatural in the way that homosexuality is," which shows he is splitting moral law from teleology rather than collapsing every sin into "unnatural."

## Why it is wiki-worthy

This is a first-principles move Lyle already uses elsewhere: do not let a word do two jobs. "Nature" as a pile of observed events cannot ground "ought." "Nature" as created order / proper function can. The same split appears whenever people argue from "it happens" to "it is therefore fitting."

## Limits

- Single-source pastoral argument. Not a literature review of biology, not a biblical-exegesis page, not a policy brief.
- Highly contested. Marked as such. Counter-arguments typically reject teleology, treat pleasure or consent as the only moral criterion, or deny that reproductive complementarity defines the act.
