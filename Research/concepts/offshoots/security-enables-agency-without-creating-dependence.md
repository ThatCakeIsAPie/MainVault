# Security Enables Agency Without Creating Dependence

> **Provide enough security for people to choose and think beyond survival, but design the security to support capability rather than permanent dependence.**

## Rationale

Formal freedom is hollow when saying no threatens a family's immediate survival. People operating under survival anxiety avoid intelligent risk, shorten their time horizon, and comply without conviction. Yet unlimited guarantees can sever reward from contribution and recreate the dependency Faleth is trying to escape.

Faleth resolves the tension through bounded security. The contribution floor removes immediate survival panic while remaining modest and eventually reconciling against future earnings. New participants receive a non-credited grace period while they learn. In acquisitions, existing compensation is honored during transition, people observe the model before choosing it, and those who decline receive severance and transition help.

Security is therefore infrastructure for agency—not a substitute for contribution.

## Examples from Practice

- Layer 1 establishes a local cost-of-living floor beneath contribution income.
- New participants receive the remainder of their first quarter plus the next quarter without creating an advance balance.
- Participants using the work as supplemental income may opt out of the floor openly.
- Acquired employees retain their prior compensation as a non-credited guarantee while their department transitions.
- Anyone declining the new model receives a real exit rather than being financially pressured into compliance.
- Bucket One in the Constitution is a personal safety base intended to make longer-term, less fearful decisions possible.

## Boundary Conditions

Security must be financially legible and bounded. If it becomes unlimited, opaque, or disconnected from productive capacity, it can consume the system it was meant to stabilize. Conversely, a nominal guarantee that leaves departure unaffordable is coercion with better branding.
