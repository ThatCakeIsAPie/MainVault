The fastest way to reduce avoidable mistakes is to learn from someone who has already walked the path rather than discovering every landmine yourself.

### Rationale

In traditional career or business paths, individuals often have to make most errors themselves before developing judgment. Network marketing systems, when run well, compress this timeline by giving newer participants direct access to experienced leaders who have already tested what works and what fails. This creates a form of "mentorship compression."

The value is not just information transfer, but pattern recognition. A good mentor helps you avoid decisions that look good on the surface but lead to dead ends.

### Examples from Practice

- New participants frequently report that having a coach who had already built teams prevented them from repeating common early mistakes around prospecting, follow-up, and team culture.
- Leaders consistently emphasize that the biggest advantage isn't the products or compensation plan, but access to people who have already solved the problems you're about to face.
- The "fifth guy in the minefield" metaphor appears repeatedly: most people would rather be the fifth person walking through a dangerous area than the first.
