# Book-Value Symmetry and Anti-Speculation

> **Use one observable valuation method in every direction so entry, exit, ownership, and separation cannot be selectively repriced.**

## Rationale

Subjective valuation creates leverage for whoever controls the negotiation. Premiums reward stories, discounts punish exits, and discretionary multiples turn ownership into a political contest.

Faleth uses book value—assets minus liabilities—as a symmetry rule. Class B shares mint and burn at book value. Capital deployment buys assets rather than speculative goodwill. A cell or subsidiary leaving settles for what it takes at book value. Someone returning comes back by the same method. The same number applies whether money or value is moving in or out.

This deliberately rejects speculative upside from multiple expansion. Wealth must come from operating businesses creating and retaining real value. The trade is less narrative-driven upside in exchange for legibility, fairness, and resistance to manipulation.

## Examples from Practice

- Minting Class B at current book value adds equity and shares proportionally, leaving existing book value per share unchanged.
- Burning shares removes equity and shares proportionally, so another holder's price is not moved by the exit.
- Investor capital is tracked as a matching asset and liability and therefore does not inflate the office's own equity.
- A Peaceful Fork uses book value rather than punitive discounts or a negotiated market multiple.
- Acquisitions and deployments are evaluated by the assets and productive value received, not merely by a seller's narrative.

## Boundary Conditions

Book value is only as trustworthy as the books. Accurate classification, impairment, liabilities, contingent obligations, and asset transfer terms remain essential. The principle removes discretionary valuation games; it does not eliminate accounting judgment or legal complexity.
