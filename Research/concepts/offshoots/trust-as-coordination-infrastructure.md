Trust is the invisible infrastructure that allows people to coordinate effort toward shared objectives without every interaction becoming expensive, defensive, or adversarial.

### Core Mechanism

Trust can be framed as: "If I invest my effort here, I will not regret it." When people believe that, they voluntarily contribute effort, attention, capital, talent, and risk. Without it, coordination costs rise because every participant must protect themselves against exploitation.

### What Trust Enables

- Teams can move faster because less energy is spent on internal protection.
- Mentors can invest in people because success is structurally aligned.
- Customers and partners can buy into a promise before the full result exists.
- Leaders can coordinate action around a future state that does not yet exist.
- Institutions can scale beyond direct personal relationships.

### Evidence Over Claims

Trust is rarely built through declarations. It compounds through visible evidence: consistency, results, transparency, time, and completed work. A portfolio of finished projects demonstrates capability more powerfully than verbal positioning.

### Connection to LTD / Network Marketing

The Tyler Sheridan session frames the business as people helping other people win: "If I win and you lose, that isn't right. If you win and I lose, that isn't right." The structure works because success is relationally and economically aligned: mentors are rewarded when others build real businesses, not when they merely extract fees from prospects. ^[Business/LTD Amway/Info Sessions/2026/2026-06-04 Tyler Sheridan.md]

### Strategic Implication

Many apparent constraints are downstream of trust. A business may think it lacks money, leads, labor, or approvals when the deeper bottleneck is that people do not yet trust the operator, the offer, the system, or the incentive structure enough to coordinate around it. ^[raw/transcripts/Trust_Effective_Effort_and_The_Factory.md]
