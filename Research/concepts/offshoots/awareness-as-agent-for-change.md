Awareness of one's own blind spots, habits, and energy is positioned as the primary driver of personal and professional transformation in leadership development contexts.

### Core Idea
"**Awareness is the greatest agent for change**" (Eckhart Tolle). Most people operate with significant unawareness about how they show up, their quirks, energy, and impact on others. This unawareness creates barriers to growth, relationships, and leadership effectiveness.

### Johari Window Application
The framework highlights four areas:
- **Open Area**: Known to self and others
- **Blind Spot**: Known to others, not self (primary target for mentorship feedback)
- **Hidden Area**: Known to self, not others (skeletons in the closet)
- **Unknown Area**: Unknown to self and others

Mentorship and community environments accelerate movement from blind spots and hidden areas into open awareness through honest, private feedback.

### Practical Implications in Network Marketing / Leadership
- People often remain unaware of behaviors like phone addiction while walking, facial expressions, or energy projection that affect first impressions and team culture.
- Self-awareness is the first step toward self-improvement; without it, individuals cannot change what they do not see.
- Leaders commit to daily awareness discussions and modeling (e.g., never on phone while walking).
- Confession or sharing hidden areas with trusted mentors releases power over past mistakes and enables change.
- Energy is felt within 6 feet; awareness of one's energy is critical for influence.
