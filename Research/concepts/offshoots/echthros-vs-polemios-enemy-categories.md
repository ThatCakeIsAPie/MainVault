# Echthros vs Polemios Enemy Categories

## Claim

@Gundamritter's 2026-08-15 quote-tweet (bookmarked) says a “I checked the original Greek” dunk still misses a real category split in Greek:

1. **πολέμιος (*polemios*):** public enemy; enemy of the *polis*; the opposing side in open conflict.
2. **ἐχθρός (*echthros*; poster's `echtros`):** private/personal enemy; the neighbor, rival, or persecutor who hates *you*.

The post's applied conclusion is that Matthew 5:44 (“love your enemies”) uses *echthroi*, so it is about personal enmity, not a command to treat public enemies of the city as if they were merely difficult neighbors. The quoted graphics are a Matthew 5:44 poster plus “Me after verifying in seven translations and the original Greek…”

The durable idea for the wiki is the **category split**, not a finished just-war treatise or a license to ignore the Sermon on the Mount.

## Why it is wiki-worthy

This is the same first-principles move as [[concepts/offshoots/nature-as-telos-not-occurrence-catalog]]: do not let one English word do two jobs. “Enemy” in English collapses a personal slight, a persecuting neighbor, a political opponent, and a public wartime foe. Those are not the same moral object.

It maps onto existing Faleth doctrine without becoming Faleth policy:

- [[faleth/governance/conflict-escalation-trust-preservation-2026]] already escalates by *scope of impact*. Personal enmity stays local; public/systemic threat does not get handled as a hallway feud.
- [[faleth/governance/peaceful-fork-voluntary-alignment-2026]] refuses to convert disagreement into exile or confiscation. That is *echthros* discipline, not *polemios* total war.
- [[concepts/offshoots/identity-and-belief-drive-behavior]] and [[concepts/offshoots/faith-anchored-action-and-obedience]]: people use “love your enemies” as a scoreboard or a shutdown slogan. The verse still binds personal hatred; it does not automatically erase civic judgment.

## Limits

- Single viral source. The poster is dunking, not doing a lexicon paper. Marked contested.
- Classical *echthros* is not a sealed “private only” box. It can mean the hated person *or* a hostile party in war. The Greek of Matthew 5:43–44 is *ἐχθρούς*; it is compatible with a personal reading and is not, by itself, a knockdown proof that public enemies are out of scope.
- The Latin reception is cleaner than the Greek: the Vulgate uses *inimicus* (private enemy), not *hostis* (public enemy). Early just-war debate often turned more on “you shall not kill” than on this verse.
- Inverse error is also real: treating a neighbor's insult as if they were an enemy of the *polis*. Category discipline cuts both ways.
- Do not harden this bookmark into Faleth sanctioning rules or foreign-policy theology.
