# Purple Cow

> **Go after the hard, never-done-before thing. The ordinary gets scrolled past. Attention is winner-take-all.**

A business and brand principle, not just a content tip. Distilled from a MrBeast clip on The Diary of a CEO, where he uses Seth Godin's Purple Cow analogy. Lyle shared the clip on 2026-11-22 and asked for it to be filed as a principle. ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-x-share-2124459878408433851.md]

## The idea

If you're driving and you see a cow, you don't stop. If you see a purple cow, you stop, tell your friends, and still think about it years later. MrBeast applies the same split to ideas. Seen-before work gets scrolled past, the same way you ignore a cow by the road. The ideas that get watched are the ones that make people go: I've never seen that. I have to look. ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-x-share-2124459878408433851.md]

Those ideas are usually hard. If something has never been done, there's usually a reason. The work is to train yourself not to resent difficult, original problems, and to run toward them. ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-x-share-2124459878408433851.md]

Attention at the top is winner-take-all. He puts it this way: it's way easier to get 50 million views on one video than a million views on 50 videos. So lean into the purple-cow effect. ^[raw/transcripts/lyle-x-share-2124459878408433851.md]

## Lyle's take

Not just a content rule. A general way to approach business and brand: go after the really hard things. Especially in marketing, do something so wild and out there that people can't help but look. He called that a massive, massive thing.
