The highest-leverage builders focus less on the visible product and more on the factory that repeatedly produces products, teams, leaders, and future capacity.

### Core Distinction

A product generates revenue. A factory generates products. Therefore, the deeper game is not merely producing one successful output; it is building the system that can produce outputs repeatedly without requiring the original operator's direct effort each time. ^[raw/transcripts/Trust_Effective_Effort_and_The_Factory.md]

### Examples

- A sales process is a factory for contracts.
- A sales team is a factory for sales processes.
- A leadership system is a factory for sales teams.
- A culture is a factory for leaders.

### Why This Matters

People often optimize the visible artifact because it is easier to measure. Owners optimize the production system because that is where compounding leverage lives. A company with a strong factory can keep generating, improving, and replacing products. A person with only a product must keep personally pushing output.

### Network Marketing Application

Tyler Sheridan's "leveraged leadership income" framing points to the same mechanism: build it once, then earn as others win. The business scales only when other people become capable owners, not when the original builder remains the heroic individual performer. ^[Business/LTD Amway/Info Sessions/2026/2026-06-04 Tyler Sheridan.md]

### Strategic Implication

For Faleth Capital and subsidiary building, the question should often move one layer upstream: not "what product are we shipping?" but "what repeatable factory creates that product, develops operators, earns trust, and improves without constant manual force?"
