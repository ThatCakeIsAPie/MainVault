# Spar distillate — One Billion Homes: how it starts, and Lyle's role (2026-11-23)

Source: Spar, closing distillate of the 2026-11-23 conversation with Lyle (same conversation as `spar-2026-11-23-dfw-build-cost-explorer-home-construction.md` and `spar-2026-11-23-one-billion-homes-compensation-drip.md`). His words and conclusions only.

## Correction to Spar's earlier note
- He has no equity in VXE. His LLC is technically a contractor for VXE and he earns by commission. (Earlier that day he called himself a partner, not the owner; this is the precise version.)
- His concern: so much at VXE is outside his control that he does not know what his best activity is right now.

## One Billion Homes: how it starts
- One cell first, not a whole home.
- Before One Billion Homes, his idea was to use about $100-200k to start an electronics manufacturer built around the system. He now wants One Billion Homes instead and does not want to keep jumping between intermediary businesses.
- The way he frames it: "what has to happen from here to there."
- Likely path: start as a subcontractor for a general contractor, then use the resources from VXE and the experience in the bottleneck to move into a general contractor role themselves.
- If VXE could make $1M+ instead of $100k+, he sees that as genuinely useful long term.

## His role (his conclusion at the end)
- He does not need to build the entire business himself. He needs to gather the team that will make the business, and secure the funding and the compensation plan.
- So the highest-leverage activity could be networking and finding the people. Someone might even know where he can get the first few tasks underway.
- Option he named: hire or partner with an experienced project manager who understands his vision and dream; go to networking events, find people who could be subs, and get in touch with companies that could help raise money.

## Open questions he left
- He lacks experience and would not want to be a leader who does not know how to do the work, or at least understand it. But he questions how important that really is.
- He worries that getting hands-on "experience" in the industry could teach him the wrong lesson.
- Whether networking and team-gathering beats getting hands-on himself. He leaned toward networking but did not close it.
