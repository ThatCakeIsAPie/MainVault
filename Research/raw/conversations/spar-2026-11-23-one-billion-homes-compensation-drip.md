# Spar distillate — One Billion Homes compensation and "the drip" (2026-11-23)

Source: Spar, follow-on from the same 2026-11-23 conversation as the DFW Build Cost Explorer handoff (see `raw/conversations/spar-2026-11-23-dfw-build-cost-explorer-home-construction.md`). Lyle's own conclusions only. He called the numbers a hypothetical example, not settled parameters.

## What stood out to him in the explorer

- Taking out land, fees and the like, there is about a $400k gap between materials cost and the final home price, and labor is only about 39% of that gap.
- Even that labor figure is high compared with what the workers actually make per hour.
- He wants to find out what the part the model cannot explain really is.

## Compensation stack he described

- Bring all the work in-house.
- A floor for everyone, maybe starting non-credited.
- Do more than the bare minimum and earn well above the floor.
- Profit share on top. A project manager he knows in the industry told him nobody he knows of offers profit share, so it would attract and keep talent.
- Company trucks and tools.

## New idea: the drip

Differs from the current quality holdback in `Business/Faleth Capital/One Billion Homes/One_Billion_Homes.md` section 2, which is "a small share" released at the one-year walkthrough.

- Take about half of what someone earns for a completed build and pay it out monthly over a year, as long as there are no callbacks. A callback cuts it off.
- Purpose, in his words: primarily quality. Second, it "silently gets people hooked on recurring income," which should lead them to Class B shares that pay dividends, then toward building systems that generate income on their own, and wealth for themselves and their families.
- Who loses it on a callback: the people whose stage failed lose the drip for that home. If the problem is deeper and other departments could have caught it, everyone loses it.
- His example: an apprentice electrician earns $4,000 for a completed home. $2,000 is paid in Layer 2, $2,000 drips at about $167 a month for a year. At 4 to 5 homes a month the drips stack, and they decay over time if the person stops working.
- He is not married to keeping the Layer 2 cap at 40 hours a week.

## Drip to Class B

- Everyone gets gain share anyway and has a choice: take it or mint it. Most will start by taking. Over time, especially as they get around people who are minting, they realize they could mint and build equity, and they transition over.
