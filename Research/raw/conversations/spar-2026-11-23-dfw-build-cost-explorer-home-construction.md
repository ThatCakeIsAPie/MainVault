# Spar distillate — DFW build cost explorer and the home-construction path (2026-11-23)

Source: Spar, from its conversation with Lyle on 2026-11-23. Lyle asked Spar to hand this to Wiki Drop. Everything under "Lyle's words" is Lyle's own view, not external fact.

## The attachment

- File: `DFW Build Cost Explorer.html`, one self-contained interactive page (about 110 KB).
- Kept unchanged in the vault at `raw/documents/dfw-build-cost-explorer-2026-11-23.html` (sha256 of the file `d1b03426e3bdadb25cdd937752c2445c59c2ddb5735b9f2769d70c43b6fe19f0`).
- Claude built it for Lyle so he could understand what it costs to build a house.
- It starts from the NAHB 2024 survey average home ($665,298 sale price, 2,647 sq ft) and drills down: sale price, then construction cost, then 8 stages, then each line item, then a takeoff of quantity times unit price with a source and a confidence level per row.
- Underneath is a bottom-up model of one reference home in Dallas-Fort Worth, priced November 2026.
- Tabs: Breakdown, Opportunities (lines that are large, labor-heavy, or priced far above their materials), Model notes (reference home spec, labor rates, limits, sources).
- Caveat stated on the page itself: the survey figures are published, but many unit prices are the model's own estimates and should be replaced with supplier quotes.

## Lyle's words and conclusions

- VXE is in a slow season. His daily work dropped from about 4 hours to about 30 minutes because of the automations and systems they built. He is antsy and wants to use the time.
- Procurement deals are falling out of favor for them: margins are tight and "tons of middlemen" eat from the same slice.
- An automation-consulting offshoot is not something any of them want, short term or long.
- Faleth Capital is what he really wants to move into. Good VXE income would help fund it. The first subsidiary he wants to start is home construction under One Billion Homes.
- He is not against raising money for it, but does not think they necessarily need to.
- Plan for this season: reconnect with his Habitat for Humanity contacts and get on a few job sites for first-hand experience. He is also considering a project manager role at another builder for a contract or season, to get paid to learn and "feel the pain more."
- Open question he left: he is somewhat afraid of picking up the "industry standard" habits he wants to improve on, and is not sure whether that is a valid concern.
- Constraint right now: no car until his first VXE paycheck, which comes after the warehouse move.
