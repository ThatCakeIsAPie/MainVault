## Long text (note_tweet)

Whenever I say that homosexual acts are contrary to nature, somebody inevitably responds:

“But homosexuality occurs in the animal kingdom!”

Well, hold on.

First, animals do sometimes engage in stranger behavior. But people often read modern human categories back into animal behavior. An animal mounting another animal of the same sex doesn't necessarily mean that we have discovered a gay dog. Researchers distinguish temporary behavior from enduring sexual orientation or identity. Animal sexual behavior can involve dominance, social bonding, competition, excitement, mistaken identification, and all sorts of other things.

But there is a much bigger problem with the argument.

When Christians say something is “contrary to nature,” we do not mean, “This behavior has never occurred anywhere in the natural world.”

Lots of bizarre things occur in nature. Dogs eat poop. Some animals eat their young. Animals sometimes kill each other over mates. A male lion may force a female lion to mate.  The fact that an animal does something doesn't tell you that a human being ought to do it.

“Natural” means according to the nature of the thing. What is it? What is it made for? What is the proper end, or telos, of its faculties?

And with human sexuality, the answer isn't exactly hidden. Men and women have complementary bodies. Male and female reproductive organs are ordered toward one another. Sexual intercourse is the kind of union that can produce children. Our very bodies testify to what sex is and what it is for.

That is what we mean when we say homosexual acts are contrary to nature. The argument isn't: “That it never occurs in the world.” The argument is:

“Look at what man and woman are.”

Nature isn't merely a catalogue of everything that happens. Nature tells us what a thing is, and from what a thing is we can discern what it is for.
