## Article plain text

I find most advice about “building your personal network” pretty useless. I think you do too.

So I wrote 15 pieces of tactical advice related to helping you build a better network that I think you’ll actually find helpful. Helpful as in it’ll help you achieve your goals faster.

1. **Stop asking to “pick people’s brains.”** Be intentional and specific about what you want and why you are reaching out; abstract requests create work and are hard to answer.
2. **Make it easy for people to help you.** Reduce the friction in requests, including introduction requests, by doing as much of the work as possible.
3. **Send thank-you emails.** Keep them short and mention a specific detail to show attention and appreciation.
4. **Don’t talk badly about other people.** Gossip signals that you may gossip about the listener too and erodes trust.
5. **Aim to actually be helpful.** Enter relationships looking for ways to be useful rather than merely extracting value.
6. **Practice active listening.** Clarify what people mean and understand what they are striving toward so you can genuinely assist.
7. **Beware of interrupting.** Let people—especially strangers—finish their thoughts.
8. **Remember small details.** Record interests, origins, and other details that create future connection points.
9. **Ask unique questions.** Move past generic small talk; ask why and be specifically curious.
10. **Hold judgments.** Delay categorizing people so you can understand and appreciate them more accurately.
11. **Keep people top of mind.** Maintain a list and send relevant essays, events, or introductions when something reminds you of them.
12. **Beware of overpromising and underdelivering.** Downplay status and qualify uncertain promises rather than damaging trust.
13. **Be consistent.** Repeated behavior over time becomes reputation; consistency is rare and memorable.
14. **Find optimism in other people’s work.** Poking holes is easy; look for credible ways their work could succeed.
15. **Remember where you started.** Give beginners room to be imperfect, as others likely did for you.
