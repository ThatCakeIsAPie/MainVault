# Lyle — Faleth MCA Performance Fee Flows to Class B

## Context

Telegram refinement of the Faleth Capital financial framework.

## Lyle's realization

The 20% performance fee the family office collects from investors goes economically to Class B shareholders rather than Class A members. Class A membership provides governance, not a direct financial claim on the office fee. Class A members make money through the subsidiaries they run and through any Managed Capital Accounts they personally hold.

That means an MCA investor is not merely investing in a pool of assets. The investor is backing the people doing the work of building, deploying, and operating those assets. When investor capital performs, the performance fee materially increases both the dividend income and the share growth of the Class B holders.

## Mechanism

- Investor earns their proportional share of pool growth.
- Faleth charges 20% of that investor-side growth as a performance fee.
- The fee becomes office income.
- Thirty percent of office-side gains is distributed to Class B as dividends.
- The retained seventy percent increases office equity and therefore Class B book value per share.
- Class A status receives none of this fee merely for governing.

In the existing $5 million pool example, the $48,000 performance fee alone contributes $14,400 to the Class B dividend pool and $33,600 to retained Class B book value: $1.44 of dividend and $3.36 of book-value growth per share across 10,000 shares.

## Implication

The investor/operator relationship is reciprocal: outside capital receives access to a system operated by aligned builders, while the fee paid for that performance compounds ownership for those builders. The operator incentive is not an incidental payroll expense; it is structurally where the manager economics land.
