# Lyle X Share — Peterson career vs good life trade-off

## Lyle's note

> I actually think there is a first-principles in this

## Full post text (note_tweet)

Jordan Peterson on the trade-off between a great career and a good life:

1. you can get a long way by concentrating on nothing but your career.
a minority of men do this. 70, 80 hours a week, flat out. they're staking everything on a small probability of exceptional status in one narrow domain. it works. it's also hard on them. no family, no leisure, no life outside the work. they get very one-dimensional.

2. that one-dimensionality might be the price of being exceptional.
if you want to be a genius level mathematician, or a scientist at the top, you're in the lab all the time. smart, dedicated, narrow. that's how you beat everyone else doing the same thing. it may be the only way. if you love it, more power to you. but you're going to pay for it.

3. the math is additive and brutal.
if i'm smart and hardworking and i crank out 70 hours a week, and you do 30, in two years i'm so far ahead of you that you will never catch up.

4. jung thought men go after perfection and women go after wholeness.
perfection is stake it all on one thing and look for radical success. wholeness asks a different question. do you want one thing in your life at 150 percent, or five things at 80 percent? there is a lot more richness in the five.

5. a family. something meaningful outside of work. a network of friends.
those three or four things alone are plenty to keep you oriented. and if one of them collapses, everything doesn't go. the price is that the more you optimize the balance, the less likely you are to be fantastically successful at any single one. considered across a whole life, that might still be the winning strategy.

6. most women bail out of unidimensional careers in their 30s.
they won't put in the 80 hours it takes to dominate a field. they decide it isn't worth it, and no wonder. two things drive it. socioeconomic status makes a man more attractive on the mating market and does not do the same for a woman. and a woman's timeframe is compressed. a guy can say he'll have kids later until he's 80. she can't.

7. one in three couples over 30 has fertility problems.
defined as not conceiving after a year of trying. one in three. wanting children and not getting them is one of the common routes to extreme unhappiness, and if it happens to you, you're in the medical mill for ten years. worth thinking about before it's the thing you're thinking about.
