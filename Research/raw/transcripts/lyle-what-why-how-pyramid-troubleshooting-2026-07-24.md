# Lyle's What–Why–How Pyramid as a Troubleshooting Fault Tree

> There we go. I feel like it also is the best trouble shooting mechanism:
>
> Cant get yourself to do anything? Check your why. You might know what you are avoiding and what pulls you, but if you hate the process like the plague, you wont do it. Or you are doing a ton of... stuff, but you aren't getting results, it is because you are lacking in at least one of the resources (usually talent, as talent is the one skill that never truly goes down, with time never truly being replenishable and the most valuable, and money being the one everyone talks about but is the easiest to get and lose)

## Distilled claims

- The pyramid doubles as a troubleshooting fault tree.
- Inaction despite a known outcome points first to Why, including a Process that may be intolerable even when Push and Pull are clear.
- High activity without results points first to How: one or more of Time, Talent, or Treasure is missing, poorly allocated, or converting inefficiently.
- Time is nonrenewable and therefore the most intrinsically scarce resource.
- Talent is comparatively durable and cumulative, making it the most common high-leverage constraint.
- Treasure is highly liquid and salient, but also comparatively acquirable and losable.
