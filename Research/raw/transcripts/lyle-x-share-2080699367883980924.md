# Garry Tan — AI Microproductivity Requires Organizational Redesign

## Shared post

> To get macro productivity gains, managers and CEOs have to greenlight radically different staffing and workflow plans
>
> To date I don’t think they’ve done it
>
> Be prepared for this to take 10 years not 2

— [Garry Tan](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2080699367883980924), quoting Ruxandra Teslo.

## Quoted argument

Ruxandra Teslo argued that intelligence is often not the primary real-world bottleneck. Her examples included medical progress constrained by regulation and clinical trials, and housing constrained by political will despite long-available construction technology.

She connected that thesis to new Stripe Economics analysis: measurable AI gains on bounded tasks have not yet become the macroproductivity gains many expect because organizational diffusion and complementary redesign remain hard.

## Stripe Economics evidence

Ernie Tedeschi’s July 23, 2026 analysis, “AI and productivity,” reported:

- Prior studies found meaningful micro-level gains: customer-service agents about 14% more productive; writers about 40% faster with improved quality; consultants completing about 12% more tasks with about 25% less time per task; and knowledge workers saving about two email hours per week.
- US labor productivity was running around 2.5% year-over-year versus a 1.6% annual average over the prior two decades, with the paper’s Markov-switching model estimating a 93% probability of a high-productivity regime.
- However, San Francisco Fed TFP estimates were near zero over the prior year; BLS estimated 0.8% TFP growth in 2025, roughly its prior ten-year average rather than an extraordinary acceleration.
- The weak industry-level association between AI adoption and recent productivity disappeared after controlling for those industries’ pre-pandemic productivity trends.
- Bank of Korea research found generative AI reduced work time about 3.8%—roughly 1.5 hours weekly—but those savings had essentially no relationship with realized output unless workers had autonomy or strong incentives to reallocate the time productively.
- Software gains attenuated from commits and code volume through pull requests, projects, and shipped releases because review, integration, testing, release, discovery, and adoption remained downstream human bottlenecks.
- Recent aggregate US productivity gains appeared more attributable to firms running existing capital harder—greater utilization of factories, servers, GPU clusters, and other installed capacity—than to AI task gains already diffusing through whole organizations.

The analysis explicitly treated this as a timing and organizational-transmission finding, not evidence that AI lacks value. Firms may gradually reorganize workflows, incentives, and complementary processes as they did after prior technological shocks.

## Lyle / pyramid connection

This maps directly to [[research/queries/what-why-how-three-by-three-action-architecture]]:

- **What:** macro organizational output.
- **Local How improvement:** AI increases available Talent and reduces Time required for bounded tasks.
- **Observed symptom:** more local output without proportional shipped or aggregate results.
- **Fault-tree diagnosis:** inspect the rest of How—staffing, integration, review, allocation, complementary Talent, and use of saved Time and Treasure.
- **Cross-branch repair:** autonomy and incentives belong to the motivational/governance side; they determine whether Time saved by new Talent gets productively redeployed.
- **Apex validation:** faster subtasks are irrelevant if the final What does not improve.

It also reinforces [[concepts/offshoots/factory-over-product-thinking]]: improving one workstation is not the same as redesigning the factory.
