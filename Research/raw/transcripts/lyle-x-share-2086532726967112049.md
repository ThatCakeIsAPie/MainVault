## What the exchange is asking

ClipProj replaces MiniMax H3's Qwen3-VL-32B text encoder with Qwen3-VL-4B and learns a linear map from the smaller encoder's hidden states into the conditioning space expected by H3. The reply asks why the original 32B encoder is not instead compressed with PrismML's Bonsai 1-bit or ternary method.

## Initial interpretation

The ideas solve related but different problems. ClipProj is a publicly reproducible, task-specific encoder substitution plus a small trained adapter. Bonsai is a model-specific low-bit weight release and runtime stack. PrismML says its method is architecture-agnostic, but no public Bonsai checkpoint or conversion tool exists for MiniMax H3's Qwen3-VL-32B text encoder. Applying Bonsai would require PrismML or another team to produce and validate that exact compressed encoder and integrate compatible low-bit kernels/loaders into ComfyUI.

A Bonsai-compressed copy of the original 32B encoder could be the better engineering target because it might preserve more of the representation H3 was trained against. That remains a hypothesis until someone releases the checkpoint and compares prompt adherence, multimodal conditioning, speed, total memory, and output quality against both the original encoder and ClipProj.
