# Lyle — Marketplace Discipleship and “It’s Just Business”

## Exact Source Turn

> Yeah, that is pretty much the phrase behind everything. I've stated that my own personal conviction is to eliminate the phrase "it's just business" from the American business vocabulary, as it is just the immature coward's way of saying that they don't care about people and worship money as their god. That has bred a desire to truly bring discipleship dynamics into the marketplace, in all industries. It is why I have been so attracted to NM, but also my biggest frustration with it is that why on earth is it subject purely to the sales and marketing industry and escapes to nowhere? All in all, it really comes back down to that phrase, and it is my own belief that making that phrase your lived commandment is the key to true fulfillment.

## Distilled Delta

“Equip people until they no longer need you” is not merely a Faleth leadership technique. Lyle treats it as a **lived marketplace commandment** and a candidate principle for every industry.

The phrase “it’s just business” represents the opposite moral architecture: an attempt to separate commercial action from human responsibility, excuse indifference to people, and make money the final authority. Faleth is intended to prove that economic effectiveness and discipleship dynamics can reinforce one another rather than occupying separate worlds.

Network marketing attracts Lyle because its best form structurally rewards developing people until they can reproduce capability without dependence. His frustration is that this dynamic is typically confined to sales and marketing organizations instead of being translated into operations, manufacturing, services, capital stewardship, governance, and the broader marketplace.
