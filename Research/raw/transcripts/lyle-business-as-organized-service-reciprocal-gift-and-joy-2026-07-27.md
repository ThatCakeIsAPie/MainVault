# Lyle — Business as Organized Service, Reciprocal Gift, and Joy

## Exact Source Turn

> I would take it a step further, and that business is not only still human, but business done right is one of the greatest expressions of joy a man can know.
>
> At its root fundamentals, as stated in Beyond the Wage, a money is made when one person adds value to the life of another, and the other gives back by paying them money, which can be used to purchase just about anything they could need. A business is just an organization of people doing that, at a larger scale. Like simplifying a fraction, that means a business is fundamentally adding value to the lives of others. What greater joy in life is there than that? To see someone smile because of something you did for them? We have a love language for that, and it is called an act of service, or even giving gifts. The other person just gets the satisfaction of giving a gift back in the form of money. But if you make money the only thing you care about, you cut out the most vital element to satisfaction, and that is bringing joy to the lives of others.

## Distilled Delta

Business is not merely morally accountable human activity. Done rightly, it is one of the greatest available expressions of human joy.

The irreducible exchange is:

1. One person improves another person's life.
2. The recipient returns value through money.
3. Money preserves the giver's freedom to exchange that gratitude for whatever they or their family need.

A business is simply this reciprocal act organized among more people and repeated at larger scale. Simplifying the structure back to its root leaves **organized value-giving**.

Acts of service and gift-giving are recognized expressions of love. In a healthy commercial exchange, the provider experiences the joy of serving and the customer experiences the dignity and satisfaction of reciprocating with a highly portable gift: money. Profit can measure and extend the exchange, but cannot substitute for its human meaning.

When money becomes the sole object, the business removes the source of fulfillment from its own activity. It may retain revenue while hollowing out the joy produced by seeing another person's life improve.
