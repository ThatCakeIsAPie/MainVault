What allowed a population boom — for most of human history, parents took care of kids until the kids could take care of themselves, and the kids that were taking care of themselves then in turn took care of their parents. People's retirement strategy WAS kids. Now we have 401Ks and social security for retirement income, the genesis of the 40/40/40 plan, which meant that it wasn't your kids, but other people's kids. That, and when child labor laws were put in place, kids provide no real economic value in the home and are primarily something to be taken care of. When they grow up now, they usually work on striking out on their own and building their skills, but there is no expectation of them providing for their parents in kind, as their parents provided for them when children, because the retirement expectation arrived as a result of what was discussed prior. We have effectively engineered every incentive to not have kids, as they add nothing but take everything, looking again at it from a purely economic lens.

Church-going folk have more kids more as a cultural reason — a value they deem important — not because there is economic value in doing so. That cultural value itself becomes a kind of incentive, though a weak one. Individuals are unpredictable, groups are very predictable, and money is one of the greatest incentives of all.
