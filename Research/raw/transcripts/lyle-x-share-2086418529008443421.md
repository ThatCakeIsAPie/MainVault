## What was tested

The public benchmark used 30 simulated users, 1,579 sessions, 71,060 dialogue turns, and 3,750 questions per provider. Questions tested three conflict types:

1. **Dynamic:** a fact genuinely changes over time.
2. **Static:** a later planted false claim must be rejected rather than accepted as an update.
3. **Conditional:** multiple preferences can all be true, but only under their stated conditions.

Correct answers scored +1, partial answers +0.5, blank or uncertain answers 0, and wrong assertions -1. The macro score weights the three conflict categories equally.

## Headline results

| Provider | Macro score | Wrong-answer rate | Strongest use | Main weakness |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Honcho | 0.477 | 7.3% | Personal histories and changing facts | Expensive, broad derived context can contradict itself |
| mem0 | 0.392 | 19.1% | Conditional preferences | ADD-only storage leaves stale facts competing with updates |
| Supermemory | 0.288 | 16.7% | Conditional recall | Unstable self-hosted releases; empty ranked recall in 904/3,750 questions |
| Hindsight | 0.281 | 14.9% | Evolving facts at lower workload than Honcho | Very wide recall can bury facts; weak conditional memory |
| RetainDB server | 0.270 | 13.0% | No clear category lead | Required substantial patching; temporal and tenant-isolation defects |
| OpenViking | 0.132 | 7.0% | Self-contained deployment | Low wrong rate mostly came from 67.1% blank answers |
| Mnemosyne | 0.116 | 6.3% | Minimal infrastructure and token workload | 50.1% blank rate; negative static-conflict score |

Honcho produced the highest correct share at 60.0%. Its wrong-answer rate rose from 4.3% in sessions 6–10 to 7.8% in sessions 46–50. Every provider degraded as histories grew.

## Important limitations

- The benchmark tested seven specific self-hosted configurations, not every hosted edition or every possible configuration.
- The Hermes built-in curated memory, Obsidian, and GBrain were not benchmarked.
- Only automatic ingestion and automatic pre-answer context injection were tested. Agent-callable search and recall tools were explicitly untested.
- Honcho was tested with bounded hybrid automatic context and forced Dream consolidation after every session. Lyle's deployment uses tools-only recall, so the result is relevant evidence but not a direct score for our configuration.
- The answer model was Qwen3.5-4B and the judge was Gemma 4 12B. Some benchmark-designated “wrong” answers were reasonable attempts to reconcile ambiguous or contradictory histories.
- The benchmark does not test production security, durability, access control, document retrieval, multi-user isolation, vendor support, or human inspectability.

## Delta synthesis for Lyle's stack

The benchmark validates the direction of the current layered architecture rather than arguing for a provider migration:

- **Hermes built-in memory:** compact, intentionally curated standing facts and corrections.
- **Honcho:** broad personal-history reconstruction and cross-session profile memory.
- **Obsidian:** inspectable, editable, portable source of truth.
- **GBrain:** search, synthesis, and graph retrieval over the human-readable corpus.

The key risk is not “which single provider wins?” It is **conflict arbitration between layers**. Honcho's current history already demonstrates the benchmark's failure mode: stale observations about Vox and Free Range Repair coexist with later corrections. Therefore recent explicit corrections and canonical vault pages must outrank older derived summaries; conflicting evidence should be surfaced rather than silently blended into a plausible biography.

The tools-only Honcho configuration may avoid the benchmarked ~25,000-token automatic context injection and reduce irrelevant derived-context poisoning, but it transfers responsibility to Delta to actually call targeted recall tools and compare results against curated memory and canonical vault sources.

## Operational decision

**No provider migration.** Keep the layered stack. Improve it through:

1. explicit source precedence: current user statement → canonical vault page → compact Hermes memory → Honcho derived history;
2. contradiction-aware answers that expose uncertainty rather than inventing reconciliation;
3. targeted Honcho search/context calls instead of indiscriminate full-profile injection;
4. periodic cleanup of stale compact-memory entries and stale Honcho conclusions/cards;
5. evaluation of the combined stack on Lyle-specific changing facts and conditional preferences, because the public benchmark did not test this architecture.
