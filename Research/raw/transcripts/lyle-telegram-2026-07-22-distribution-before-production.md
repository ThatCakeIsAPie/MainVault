# Lyle — Distribution Before Production

## Context

Lyle connected Elon Musk's advice about sequencing ventures by capital intensity to the early Manscaped story. The example is user-supplied and should be independently verified before being cited publicly as corporate history.

## Raw reflection

> That story kind of reminds me of the story of, or that last point, or reminds me of the story of Manscaped. Because Manscaped originally started off as just a drop shipping company, but they just dropped ship just razors that were good for Manscaping, right? But the thing is that they actually started to do really well, and so they actually reinvested a lot of that into actually developing their own product line. And then over time, they just kept improving the product line, and now they're like a very well-known, very reputable brand.
>
> But I find it kind of funny because I've often said that business, you can really summarize basically, it's just the dynamics of supply and demand pretty much. It's just because the reason sales and marketing is so needed is because the supply doesn't know the demand, and the demand doesn't know the supply.
>
> And so if you can start off with that, you can just make money just by helping connect the supply to the demand. You can absolutely do that. That's what retail does. That's what so many businesses are based off of, so many sales marketing companies are based off of that. They're based off of things like distribution, and it takes no capital to really do distribution, it just takes effort.
>
> But once you start to make enough money, then you can actually move forward to actually creating the supply. And so that's one of the things I think is actually super fascinating.

## Distilled claim

Distribution can serve as a capital-light apprenticeship for production: first connect existing supply to unserved demand through effort, then reinvest both margin and customer knowledge into proprietary supply.
