# Lyle Cole — Theological Horticulture and Heart Upstream of Identity

Source: Conversation with Lyle Cole, 2026-06-04.

Lyle liked the phrase: "Religion tries to force fruit onto a tree. Relationship changes the root, and then fruit becomes the natural output. Duct-taping apples to a dead branch does not make it an apple tree."

He identified this as a strong explanation for why behavior monitoring has flaws. Repeated behavior can influence identity because the more someone does something, the more they may begin to identify with it. However, if the action is consistent but the heart is not actually in it, the behavior will continue to feel like an uphill battle.

The deeper principle: the heart is upstream of identity. To make durable change, the heart must actually begin craving the thing. Otherwise identity and behavior are being pulled uphill by force rather than flowing naturally from the root.
