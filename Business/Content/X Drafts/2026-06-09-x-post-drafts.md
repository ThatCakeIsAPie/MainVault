### 8. Thread — building a GovCon opportunity radar before buying software
Status: draft
Category: new territory
Source: `GovCon Opportunity Radar.md`, Last30Days GovCon report

```text
1/ I’m thinking through a lightweight GovCon “opportunity radar.”

Not a magic proposal writer.

A simple system that helps small teams stop missing good-fit opportunities and stop wasting time on bad-fit ones.

2/ The first job is discovery.

Monitor the right sources for target agencies, keywords, NAICS/PSC codes, set-asides, due dates, and contract types.

If the opportunity never enters the system, nothing else matters.

3/ The second job is fit scoring.

Before writing anything, answer:
- Is this actually in our lane?
- Do we have the required past performance?
- Is the due date realistic?
- Is teaming required?
- Is the compliance burden worth it?

4/ The third job is a bid/no-bid memo.

A one-page memo beats a vague “this looks interesting.”

Decision quality improves when the reasoning is written down.

Annoying, yes. Also useful. Tragic how that works.

5/ The fourth job is compliance extraction.

What must be included?
What clauses matter?
What formats are required?
What would make the proposal nonresponsive?

AI can help here, but only if every output stays tied to source text.

6/ Proposal drafting should come later.

If the system cannot reliably find, filter, and structure the opportunity, asking it to write the proposal is premature.

That is how you get confident nonsense at government-contracting speed.

7/ The general principle:

Automate after the manual loop teaches you what matters.

Otherwise you are not automating a process.
You are decorating uncertainty.
```
