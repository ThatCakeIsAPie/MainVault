### 1. Short Post — Principles vs parameters
Status: draft
Category: new territory
Source: `Faleth Capital Constitution.md` + `The Contribution Framework.md`

```text
A good operating system separates principles from parameters.

Principles should be hard to violate:
- transparency
- earned upside
- voluntary participation
- clean books
- no trapping people

Parameters should be easy to adjust:
- rates
- point values
- thresholds
- processes

Most organizations do the opposite.
They freeze the spreadsheet and negotiate the soul.
```

Category: new territory

### 8. Thread — A business should make the right behavior obvious
Status: draft
Category: new territory
Source: `The Contribution Framework.md` + `Faleth Capital Constitution.md`

```text
1/ I’m working through a compensation/governance model around one simple question:

What if the business made the right behavior obvious?

Not through surveillance.
Not through motivational posters.
Through structure.

2/ In a normal wage model, the incentives are weird.

Hourly pay rewards time spent.
Salary rewards staying inside the box.
Bonuses are often discretionary and political.
Ownership is usually reserved for the few.

Then everyone acts shocked when people do not feel like owners.

3/ A better system has to connect contribution to reward directly.

For each unit of work:
- what value was created?
- what gross margin did it produce?
- who contributed to which process?
- how is the labor pool divided?

Make the math visible.

4/ It also needs a floor.

People in survival mode do not think long-term.
They do not take productive risks.
They protect themselves, because they have to.

A modest guarantee can remove fear without turning the system back into a time clock.

5/ Then you need long-term alignment.

Weekly output matters.
But so does reputation, training, cleanliness, retention, customer trust, and whether the whole portfolio is healthy.

That is where profit share and equity-like upside matter.

6/ The hardest part is not the spreadsheet.

The hard part is separating principles from parameters.

Principles:
- transparency
- earned upside
- voluntary participation
- clean books
- no trapping people

Parameters:
- rates
- point values
- thresholds
- processes

7/ Parameters should change as reality changes.

If AI makes a process easier, recalibrate it.
If a role stops mattering, remove or reclassify it.
If a new bottleneck appears, add it.

A living system beats a sacred org chart.

8/ The goal is not to make people dependent on the company.

The goal is to develop people until they can build without you.

A leader who makes themselves unnecessary has done the job.
A system that can survive that has been built correctly.
```
