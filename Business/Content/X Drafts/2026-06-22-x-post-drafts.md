### 8. Thread — The boring workflow is the AI moat
Status: draft
Category: natural extension
Source: Last30Days debrief + GovCon/agent reports + public learning note

```text
1/ I’m becoming convinced the best AI opportunities in normal businesses are not the flashiest ones.

They are the boring recurring workflows that everyone already knows are painful.

2/ Example pattern:

A repeated input comes in.
Someone has to classify it.
Someone has to extract the important parts.
Someone has to decide what matters.
Someone has to create the next action.
Someone has to remember what happened.

3/ That could be customer follow-up, proposal intake, repair triage, recruiting, bookkeeping cleanup, vendor outreach, or market monitoring.

The industry calls this “agents.”
Most operators call it “stuff we keep dropping because humans are busy.”

4/ The mistake is jumping straight to autonomy.

The first useful version is usually:
- monitor
- summarize
- score
- draft
- route
- log
- ask for approval

Not “let the robot run the company,” because apparently we enjoy learning obvious lessons through lawsuits.

5/ GovCon makes this very clear.

AI can help watch opportunities, parse RFPs, generate compliance matrices, draft outlines, and organize source evidence.

But if it touches regulated data or submissions, the evidence trail and human approval are not optional decorations.

6/ Same principle applies everywhere:

The moat is not the model.
The moat is the operating loop around the model.

Clear input.
Clear output.
Clear owner.
Clear permissions.
Clear logs.
Clear next decision.

7/ The more I build with AI, the more I think “workflow design” is the unsexy superpower.

A model can generate text.
A system makes sure the right work happens repeatedly without turning the business into a casino with a chatbot interface.
```
