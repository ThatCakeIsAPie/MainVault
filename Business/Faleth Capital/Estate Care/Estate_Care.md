# Estate Care

*How Faleth's household services subsidiary buys people back their time.*
*Faleth Capital · Subsidiary Framework · Version 1.0 (Draft) · Working name*
*Runs under [[The Contribution Framework]], [[The Cell Framework]], [[The Marketing and Brand Framework]], and [[The Financial Framework]]. Sister subsidiary to [[One Billion Homes]]. The philosophy is in the [[Faleth Capital Constitution]].*

---

## 1. The Product Is Time

Estate Care looks after a household so the people in it don't have to: the lawn, the pool, the cleaning, the power washing, the filters and gutters and seasonal checks, and at the top tier, the paperwork and appointments that eat a family's evenings.

Each of those is a service. The product is what the family does with the hours they no longer spend on them. *[[Beyond the Wage]]* describes a person whose time can be taken by someone else's schedule. Estate Care is the same conviction pointed at the household: the hours outside work belong to the family, and a well-run house shouldn't consume them.

So the measure of the company isn't how many services it sells. It's how little a customer has to think about their home. A customer who never has to call us, because the crew already came, already noticed, and already handled it, is the product working.

---

## 2. Three Tiers

Customers choose how much of the household they want off their plate. Each tier is defined by what the customer stops thinking about, not by a list of services.

**Essentials.** The recurring work: lawn, cleaning, and pool on a set schedule, on one bill. The customer stops thinking about the yard and the chores.

**Full care.** Everything in Essentials, plus the house itself: filters, gutters, power washing, seasonal inspections, and coordinating repairs when a crew spots something. The customer stops thinking about the property.

**Premium.** Everything in Full care, plus a dedicated account manager who runs the household's logistics: appointments, vendors, renewals, the paperwork that piles up, and coordination with partner CPAs and attorneys ([[#7. The Household Office|§7]]). The customer stops thinking about the household.

Every tier is one subscription and one bill, scheduled by us. A customer moving up a tier gets more of their time back. A customer moving down keeps everything that matters to them and pays for nothing they don't use.

## 3. One Bill, Many Cells

Estate Care is the case [[The Cell Framework]] was written for: several kinds of work with natural seams between them, sold to one customer.

Each service line is a cell — lawn and landscape, pool, cleaning, exterior and power washing, home maintenance — running its own points, its own crews, and its own ledger. An **Account Management** cell owns the customer: the subscription, the bill, the schedule across every service line, and the follow-up when a crew finds something. The **Household Office** cell serves Premium customers.

The interfaces are simple. A service cell delivers a completed visit to standard and a note of anything it noticed. Account Management delivers a scheduled route and a customer who knows what to expect. When a crew spots a problem outside its own line — the lawn crew sees a failing gutter — the note goes to Account Management, which routes it to the cell that fixes it. The customer hears about it once, already handled.

Early on, Estate Care is one cell doing a few services well. Service lines become cells as each one outgrows a shared pool.

## 4. The Visit Is the Ticket

Under [[The Contribution Framework#3. The Value Share|the Contribution Framework]], each completed visit funds its own labor pool from its own gross margin. A visit's cost of goods is small — chemicals, supplies, plants, filters — so most of each ticket is margin the crews earn from. Household services are labor-heavy, so the cost of fulfillment rate here runs higher than in a business that sells parts, and the team sets it at calibration.

Points are earned on completion, not by the hour. A crew that finishes a route by early afternoon has earned the same as a crew that stretched it to dusk, and can go home. That's the whole idea of the model, and it fits this work better than almost any other.

**Route density is the margin.** The time between houses earns nothing. A crew that services twelve homes on one street, instead of eight spread across town, completes half again as many tickets in the same day. Account Management builds routes to be dense, and growth is aimed at filling streets before adding new ones. That is why the homes from [[One Billion Homes]] matter so much ([[#6. Homes From One Billion Homes|§6]]): they arrive a whole block at a time.

The 40-hour cap and Time on Task apply in full ([[The Financial Framework#7. Hours, Overtime, and Time on Task|the Financial Framework]]).

## 5. Referrals

Estate Care is exactly what the referral program in [[The Marketing and Brand Framework#2. The Referral Program|the Marketing and Brand Framework]] was designed for: recurring household spending that was going to go somewhere regardless.

**A referrer earns 20% of a referred customer's spend, as credits, for as long as that customer stays.** Credits come off the referrer's own bill first. Refer five customers on the same plan, and your own care is free. Refer more, and it starts paying you. Once a credit balance exceeds what the customer spends, it can be cashed out or, once the offering exists, placed in a Managed Capital Account ([[The Equity Framework#4. Managed Capital Accounts|the Equity Framework]]).

It runs one level deep and comes out of cost of acquisition, the same as any other way of winning a customer ([[The Marketing and Brand Framework#3. What It Costs|the Marketing and Brand Framework]]).

That path is the Prosumer promise made literal. A family buys back their time, tells their neighbors, stops paying for their care, and eventually owns a stake in the system that looks after their home. The referral only works if the service deserves it, so quality comes first ([[#8. Standards|§8]]).

## 6. Homes From One Billion Homes

Every home [[One Billion Homes]] builds comes with a period of free Full care.

**Each subsidiary keeps honest books.** One Billion Homes buys the care at Estate Care's normal price and books it as a cost of acquisition for selling the home. Estate Care earns full price on every visit, so nothing is hidden inside a discount.

**The free period is how customers meet the higher tier.** A new homeowner starts on Full care, sees what it's like not to think about the property, and decides what to keep when the period ends. Many keep it.

**The visits protect the guarantee.** During the home's warranty, Estate Care crews are the first to notice a slow leak, a sticking door, or a crack. Those notes go to One Billion Homes' Homeowner Care cell, which fixes the problem before it becomes a callback and feeds the cause back to the cells that built the house.

**New neighborhoods arrive dense.** One Billion Homes builds whole blocks at a time, so its homeowners give Estate Care its tightest routes from the day they move in, and every one of them is a referrer with neighbors who aren't customers yet.

## 7. The Household Office

The Premium tier is the household version of what a family office does for the wealthy: a team that is proactive on the family's behalf. Its account managers know the household, keep its calendar of renewals and deadlines, handle vendors and appointments, and step in before a problem lands on the family.

**Licensed advice stays with licensed firms.** Texas requires law firms to be owned by lawyers and CPA firms to be majority-owned by CPAs, so that advice answers only to the client. The Household Office coordinates and advocates. Partner CPAs and attorneys do the licensed work, bill the client directly, and pay Estate Care nothing for the introduction, and Estate Care takes no share of their fees. That keeps every partner's advice answerable only to the client, which is the transparency commitment in [[The Marketing and Brand Framework#4. Brand Standards|the Brand Standards]] applied to the people we bring into a customer's life.

**MCA holders are its natural first customers.** People who place capital with the office are the people most likely to want their household run this way. The Household Office stands on its own margin as a tier of Estate Care, and the office may choose to buy a tier for its account holders later if that ever makes sense. The office stays thin either way ([[Faleth Capital Constitution#2.1 — The Office|Article II]]).

## 8. Standards

Estate Care works inside people's homes, around their families, often when nobody is there. That raises the bar on the brand commitments every Faleth subsidiary makes ([[The Marketing and Brand Framework#4. Brand Standards|the Marketing and Brand Framework]]).

**Trust.** Every person who enters a customer's home is someone the team would let into their own. How the team vets and introduces its people is a team standard, set at calibration, and never lowered to fill a route.

**Quality.** The referral program only works if the service deserves referring. A missed visit or a careless job is a broken promise to the person who referred the customer, not just to the customer.

**Transparency.** Any customer can see exactly what they're paying for, what was done on each visit, and how their referral credits are calculated.

**The customer never chases us.** The schedule, the follow-up, and the fix come from our side. If a customer has to call to ask whether we came, we didn't deliver the product.

---

*This is a living framework. The service lines, the tiers and their prices, the cost of fulfillment rate, the free care period for new homes, and the cell boundaries are parameters the people doing the work set and revisit each quarter. The commitments are the parts meant to last: the product is time given back, every customer can turn their care into a stake, and a family should never have to think about a house we look after.*
