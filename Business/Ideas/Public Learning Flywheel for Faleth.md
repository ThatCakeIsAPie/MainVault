# Public Learning Flywheel for Faleth

## Core Idea

Use public content as a learning, authority, and customer-acquisition flywheel.

The operating philosophy combines:

1. **Andrej Karpathy:** take on concrete projects, learn on demand, summarize/teach what you learn in your own words, and compare only to past-you.
2. **Alex Hormozi:** do cool shit and post about it.
3. **Russell Brunson:** free content can become paid customer acquisition when the platform pays creator revenue, sponsors pay for audience access, and the content generates business leads.

For Lyle/Faleth, the strategy is not to "be a content creator" as a separate identity. The strategy is to make the real work visible:

```text
Do real projects → capture discoveries in the vault → distill into public posts → attract audience/leads/partners → improve the projects → repeat
```

## Why Now

The direct X payout is not the primary ROI. The larger ROI is building a public operating thesis around Faleth while potentially getting paid by the platform to document the work.

These are not abstract opinions. They are things being built, tested, refined, and documented.

## Content Philosophy

KISS version:

```text
Build in private enough to stay focused.
Document in the vault enough to think clearly.
Publish in public enough to attract the right people.
```

Public posts should usually come from one of four sources:

1. **Discovery:** something newly learned while solving a real problem.
2. **Decision:** a principle or tradeoff clarified through work.
3. **Artifact:** a workflow, diagram, template, note, or system created.
4. **Reflection:** how current thinking differs from past thinking.

## 12-Month Thesis

If Lyle posts consistently for a year while actually building Faleth systems, the likely compounding effects are:

- clearer thinking
- better documentation
- public proof of work
- stronger founder/operator network
- potential creator revenue
- potential business leads
- easier recruiting/collaboration
- a searchable archive of Faleth's worldview

The goal is not virality for its own sake. The goal is to become visibly useful to the exact people Faleth wants to attract.

## First Version

30-day experiment (updated 2026-08-14 for For You same-author tax + OCR):

- Workday cap: **1 original** from actual work + **8–12 thoughtful replies** in four rooms.
- VXE hell day: zero X. The feed does not get to eat fulfillment.
- Weekly: 1 thread or X Article + 1 artifact post.
- Weekly: review conversations and verified-follower quality, not raw impressions.

Money path is Original Content Rewards, not ads revenue share. See `Research/faleth/content/x-ocr-to-supergrok-prosumer-2026.md`.

Potential weekly themes:

1. Building the GovCon opportunity radar.
2. Why book-value buy/sell keeps acquisition logic simple.
3. Automating repetitive business operations with agents.
4. Turning Obsidian into an operating memory system.
5. Process motivation as a business-building edge.

## KPIs

Track:

- posts/week
- meaningful replies/week
- profile visits
- follows from target audience
- useful DMs/conversations
- leads or collaboration opportunities
- creator revenue, if eligible
- quality of vault notes created from public-writing loop

Avoid over-weighting vanity metrics. Impressions matter, but the real test is whether the content attracts useful people and clarifies Faleth's operating thesis.

## Risks

- Posting too much half-baked strategy without enough actual building.
- Turning private operational ambiguity into public noise.
- Chasing engagement instead of useful audience fit.
- Over-explaining proprietary workflows before Faleth benefits from them.
- Letting the feed replace execution.

## Guardrails

- Publish principles, lessons, and sanitized workflows; keep sensitive client/company specifics private.
- Use public content to increase execution pressure, not replace execution.
- Prefer proof-of-work posts over hot takes.
- Compare only to past Lyle, not full-time creators with five-person content teams. Yes, apparently that reminder is necessary.
