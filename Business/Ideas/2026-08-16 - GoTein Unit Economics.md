# GoTein Unit Economics

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: First-pass model, labeled assumptions
Parent: [[2026-08-16 - GoTein Protein On The Go]]

## Punchline

A 50g shake and a 100g meal can hit QSR food-cost targets. The expensive part is not the protein. The expensive part is a staffed store.

Chicken-led 100g is cheap. Whey-led 50g is currently the worse ingredient. A pickup commissary can break even on a few dozen meals a day. A strip-center restaurant cannot.

## Working dose

- Shake / grab breakfast: 50g protein
- Hero eat-now meals: 100g protein
- Shelf example: 50g breakfast burrito at $8, reheat later

Lyle refined the format on 2026-08-16: quick-stop, every SKU protein-first, hot and cold grab-and-go shelf. Not a delivery box. Not “Chipotle if you know the hack.” Parked until after VXE cash and LTD platinum or ruby.

## Ingredient math

Cooked skinless chicken breast is about 31g protein per 3.5 oz / 100g.[14]

Raw-to-cooked yield used here: 70%. That is a kitchen planning number, not a lab assay.[unverified]

Foodservice boneless breast is modeled at $2.50/lb, between an NYC wholesale print near $1.89/lb and July 2026 US city-average retail of $4.15/lb.[2][1]

WPC80 printed €26,450 per tonne at the end of May 2026.[3] Converted at 1.10 dollars per euro that is about $13.20/lb.[unverified] Instant-quality WPC80 has printed near €29,000/t, about $14.50/lb at the same FX.[3] A separate US writeup put WPI near $11/lb in the 2026 squeeze.[15]

Cost per delivered gram of protein, mid case:

- Foodservice chicken: about $0.025/g
- WPI at $11/lb: about $0.027/g
- Boom WPC80: about $0.036/g

Chicken wins the 100g plate. Whey is a blender convenience, not a cheaper gram.

### Shake, 50g

| Build | Protein $ | Other $ | Plate COGS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| WPC80 boom + milk + fruit + cup | 1.82 | 1.45 | 3.27 |
| WPI $11 + same extras | 1.35 | 1.45 | 2.80 |

Other = $0.80 milk, $0.40 fruit, $0.25 cup/lid. Planning extras, not invoices.[unverified]

Ticket to land 28–32% food cost: about $9–$12.

A $9 shake at boom whey is 36% food cost. That is fat for QSR. Price it like a meal-adjacent drink, not a $6 promo.

### Shelf burrito, 50g at $8

This is the SKU that matches the quick-stop story.

| Build | Protein $ | Wrap + foil | Plate COGS | Food cost at $8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50g chicken | 1.27 | 0.98 | 2.25 | 28% |
| 30g chicken + ~3 eggs | 1.39 | 0.98 | 2.37 | 30% |
| 25g chicken + egg white + cheese | 1.29 | 0.98 | 2.27 | 28% |

Wrap extras are planning numbers: tortilla $0.32, cheese $0.28, salsa $0.12, foil/label $0.18, oil/spice $0.08.[unverified]

At $8 this is 6.3 grams per dollar, better than My Fit Foods retail and a Chipotle 81g bowl, worse than Chipotle’s $3.82 protein cup.[6][7][17][9]

Batch labor is the real test, not the tortilla. Two people, five hours, 200 burritos: about $1.00 labor each at the loaded $20/hr used above. Food plus that batch labor is about $3.40, or 42% of $8. That still fits a grab-and-go prime-cost story if the room is a shelf, not a dining room.

$8 is a real target if chicken and eggs carry the grams. It dies if the burrito is whey-padded or sold out of a full restaurant labor load.

### Meal, 100g

All-chicken 100g is about 322g cooked meat, 1.02 lb raw.

| Build | Protein $ | Sides + box | Plate COGS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All chicken @ $2.50/lb | 2.54 | 1.30 | 3.84 |
| 70g chicken + 30g WPC80 | 2.87 | 1.30 | 4.17 |
| All chicken @ $3.00/lb | 3.05 | 1.30 | 4.35 |

Sides + box = rice/potato $0.25, veg $0.40, sauce $0.20, clamshell $0.35, label $0.10.[unverified]

Ticket to land 28–32% food cost: about $12–$15.

A $16–$18 ticket is not required by food cost. It is required by labor, rent, and the fact Chipotle already sells a double-protein bowl near $16.[17]

The 100g all-chicken plate is a pile. Eleven ounces of cooked breast is a bodybuilder plate, not a lunch bowl. If the brand promise is 100g, the kitchen either accepts that pile or folds whey / egg white / Greek yogurt into sauces and shakes so the plate still looks like food.

## Kitchen and labor

QSR / fast casual food cost target: 25–32%. Prime cost (food + labor) target: about 55–63%.[5][4]

Texas fast casual hourly is commonly $14–$18. Dallas line-cook postings cluster around $16–$21. Fast casual labor target: 28–32% of sales.[11][10]

Loaded wage used here: $16.50 × 1.22 burden = $20.13/hr. Burden is a planning factor.[unverified]

Staffed counter, 14-hour day, ~49 hourly labor-hours plus one manager at $54k:

- Daily labor: about $1,130
- 1,800 sf at $30/sf NNN plus $8 extras: about $190/day occupancy[16]
- Utilities / insurance stub: $80/day[unverified]
- Daily stand-still before food: about $1,400

At 30% food cost, that stand-still needs about $2,000/day of sales. That is 125 tickets at $16, or 167 at $12.

That $2,000 does not yet pay card fees, waste, marketing, repairs, or an owner draw. A living store is closer to $2,600–$3,000/day, call it $0.95M–$1.1M AUV. That is a real restaurant, not a side window.

Texas still allows a $2.13 tipped cash wage against $7.25, but a counter / blender concept will not run on tip credit the way a full-service dining room does.[11]

## Prep-only alternative

Two people, 8 hours, 800 sf cheap industrial:

- Daily labor + occupancy: about $490
- Hybrid 100g meal COGS about $4.17
- $16 pickup meal contribution about $11.80
- Break-even: about 40 meals/day

That is the cheap test. Same kitchen can feed a Friday gym-pickup fridge without a dining room.

## Faleth waterfall

Lyle’s target for most businesses: about 33% COG, or about 50% COG + COF, leaving about 20% COA and about 30% before fixed. That is the spoken shape of [[The Financial Framework]].

The written table is the same idea with COF defined as 20% of gross margin, which is about 13% of revenue when COG is 33%. Restaurant “food cost” is COG. Restaurant labor is not COF. COF is a pool off gross margin, not a wage line.

| SKU | Price | COG | COG % | COF (20% of GM) | COG+COF | After 20% COA |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Breakfast burrito | $8 | $2.30 | 29% | $1.14 | 43% | 37% |
| Shake, WPI | $10 | $2.80 | 28% | $1.44 | 42% | 38% |
| Shake, boom whey | $9 | $3.27 | 36% | $1.15 | 49% | 31% |
| 100g chicken meal | $16 | $3.84 | 24% | $2.43 | 39% | 41% |
| 100g chicken meal | $13 | $3.84 | 30% | $1.83 | 44% | 36% |

The $8 burrito and the $13–$16 meals sit inside his 33% COG and 50% COG+COF bands. The $9 whey shake is the one that pokes through 33% COG.

Walk-in shelf tickets can carry near-zero COA ([[The Marketing and Brand Framework]]). That 20% is room, not a bill you must spend. Delivery platforms would spend it for you.

A staffed dining room does not break COG. It breaks the 30% before fixed. Rent and idle labor are the lines that eat the waterfall after the plate is already honest.

## What the numbers actually say

1. Do not build the brand around whey grams. Whey is in a squeeze. Chicken grams are cheaper and more stable.[3][15]
2. 50g shakes work if priced $10–$12, or if whey is used as a 15–25g booster on a milk / yogurt / egg-white base.
3. 100g meals work on food cost. They fail as a tiny plate. Design the vessel for 100g, or stop promising 100g on every SKU.
4. A restaurant is a labor machine that happens to sell protein. A commissary-plus-pickup is a protein machine that happens to use a kitchen.
5. If the point is “protein, on the go,” the first unit is not a dining room. It is a 40-meal Thursday/Sunday pickup loop next to where people already train.

## Assumptions to replace with invoices

- DFW Sysco / US Foods / Restaurant Depot breast and thigh
- Actual foodservice whey or egg-white liquid, not EU WPC80 prints
- Cooked yield on the actual cut and method
- Second-gen hooded box vs ghost kitchen vs church/gym shared kitchen
- Mix: shake vs hot meal vs refrigerated prep
- Whether 100g is every SKU or a hero line

## Next useful step

Cost five real SKUs against one DFW invoice, or design the 40-meal commissary test. Do not spend more time on brand until one of those two is real.
