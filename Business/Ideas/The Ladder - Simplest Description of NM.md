# The Ladder: Simplest Description of Network Marketing

## Lyle's framing

*"It is a ladder. To go up to the next rung costs time and effort."*

The simplest way to describe network marketing — the effort structure, not the compensation structure.

## Why the ladder works (what it captures)

- **Effort, not capital/connections, is the currency.** Each rung costs time and effort. Anyone can climb; the barrier is not who you know or how much you start with.
- **Sequential and non-skippable.** One rung at a time; you must land each before the next.
- **Visible, discrete progress.** A rung is a concrete milestone — the climb is legible and motivating.
- **Grounded start.** A ladder begins on flat ground — you don't need a platform to begin.
- **Maps to the constitution**: "the barrier at every stage is effort and time rather than capital or connections."

## Where the metaphor breaks (Delta's contrarian pass)

1. **Ladders don't compound.** Arithmetic (+1 rung), not exponential. The Faleth thesis is X^Y^Z — the value of a network multiplies what's above it. A ladder's rungs don't touch each other.
2. **Solo and single-file.** You climb alone. NM is inherently multi-person: you rise *by lifting others*. A ladder has no branches, no leverage, no people.
3. **Temporary-effort.** Step off a ladder and the climb is gone. NM builds an *owned asset* that pays after you step back (the 8.4 exit: subsidiary or live on investments).
4. **No rest rung.** You must keep gripping or you fall. The graduation test is *durability* — a rung that holds without gripping.

## Synthesis

The ladder is the perfect **on-ramp** description — it answers the first two questions anyone asks ("is this a scam?" / "do I need capital?") with a single honest line: the barrier is effort and time. But it describes the *climb*, not the *asset being built*. At the top, the metaphor must shift to something that pays rent without you — that's the part the ladder can't say, and it's the whole point of the five-step blueprint.
