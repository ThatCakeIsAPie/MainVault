# Faleth Capital - Investable Wedge and Capital Confidence

Lyle clarified that the hesitation around outside capital is not fear of investors or capital itself. MCAs and Class B shares are examples of being open to external capital when the structure is aligned.

The real hesitation is pitching Faleth or a disruptive product before there is enough proof, language, and operating discipline for the idea to survive being misunderstood. The fear is not rejection alone; it is that someone hears the idea too early, does not understand it, and discards it for the wrong reason.

Current thesis:

- It is rational to want validation and operating proof before broad investor pitching, especially while coming out of debt and without a long successful business track record.
- But “verify it first” can become armor if it delays the moment when capital is the actual constraint.
- The correct question is not “am I ready to pitch all of Faleth?” but “what single wedge is concrete enough that a strategically selected investor can understand the use of funds, return mechanism, downside protection, and next proof milestone?”

Potential wedge framing:

- Use MCAs, Class B shares, or another bounded structure as the first capital product.
- Pitch a specific slice, not the whole cathedral.
- Make the ask about funding one validated operating loop, not buying into an abstract philosophy.
- Treat investor conversations as market discovery for the explanation, not as a referendum on Faleth’s worth.

Relevant contact:

- Canady Investments may be worth re-opening as a relationship-first conversation, not an immediate hard pitch.
- The first conversation should test fit, capital appetite, investor language, and what proof would make the opportunity obvious.

Possible opening frame:

“I’m not raising broadly yet. I’m tightening the model and wanted to get your investor-side read on one specific wedge before I decide whether capital is actually the constraint.”
