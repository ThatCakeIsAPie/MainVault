# Faleth Steward-Profit Framework

## Core Idea

Design Faleth Capital's ownership and compensation model as a hybrid between contribution-based profit share, steward ownership, and operator equity, without prematurely copying the complexity of ESOPs, EOTs, or formal cooperatives.

## Why Now

The 2026 employee-ownership landscape continues to show growing attention around ESOPs, Employee Ownership Trusts, worker cooperatives, profit sharing, phantom equity, and steward ownership. The common problem: founders want succession, employees want upside, and communities want companies that are not extracted by short-term capital.

Faleth's mission already points toward eliminating wage/salary dependency. The opportunity is to build a simple internal contribution framework first, then choose the legal wrapper later.

## Design Principles

- **Separate economics from control.** Profit participation does not automatically equal governance authority.
- **Reward contribution over title.** Upside should track measurable contribution, stewardship, and responsibility.
- **Preserve mission.** The company should not be easy to sell out from under the people it was built to serve.
- **Keep the first version simple.** Avoid over-lawyering before the business model and operating cadence are proven.
- **Make it legible.** Participants should understand how value is created, measured, and distributed.

## Possible Building Blocks

- Contribution points or COF-style internal accounting
- Profit-share pools by company/subsidiary
- Operator equity for accountable leaders
- Steward rights for mission/constitution protection
- Vesting or durability rules tied to contribution over time
- Clear distinction between cash distributions, retained earnings, and long-term ownership claims

## First Version

Create a plain-language model that answers:

1. Who receives upside?
2. What behavior creates upside?
3. How is contribution measured?
4. What can be paid monthly/quarterly versus retained?
5. Who governs mission-critical decisions?
6. What happens when someone leaves?
7. What happens if a subsidiary is sold, spun out, or wound down?

## Risks

- Too much complexity before revenue stabilizes
- Tax/legal ambiguity
- People confusing profit share with guaranteed income
- Governance rights becoming emotional instead of operational
- Misaligned expectations if contribution is not measured clearly

## Current Case Studies / Signals

### 2026-06-11 — P. Terry's EOT + Profit Share

P. Terry's Burger Stand is a useful live case study for Faleth's thinking: founders transferred controlling ownership to an Employee Ownership Trust and paired it with profit sharing for eligible employees, starting at 5% of operating income and aiming toward 20% over time. The important lesson is not "copy the legal wrapper tomorrow." The lesson is that ownership/control, mission continuity, tenure, profit participation, and liquidity can be designed as separate levers.
